Gynaecomastia — male breast reduction

Enlargement of male breast tissue is common, benign in the vast majority of cases, and very treatable. It still deserves a specialist assessment — both to rule out the rare serious cause and to get a result worth having.

First, a diagnosis

Gynaecomastia has many triggers: hormonal changes in adolescence and later life, weight, certain medications and occasionally underlying medical conditions. Male breast cancer is rare but real, which is why assessment by a breast specialist — with imaging and biopsy where indicated — comes before any discussion of surgery. Some gynaecomastia, particularly in teenagers, resolves on its own; some responds to treating the underlying cause.

Surgery

Where the tissue is established and troubling, surgery removes it. Depending on the balance of glandular tissue and fat, Mr Ullah uses liposuction, direct excision of the gland through a small incision at the edge of the areola, or both. Significant skin excess occasionally needs additional techniques, which he will discuss openly if they apply to you.

Recovery and results

The operation is usually a day case under general anaesthetic. A compression garment is worn for a few weeks; most men return to desk work within a week and full exercise at four to six weeks. Once mature, the result is usually permanent, provided weight and any causative medication are managed.

FAQ

Common questions

Is gynaecomastia dangerous?

Almost always no — it is benign glandular enlargement. But one-sided, firm or rapidly changing male breast tissue, or nipple discharge, should be assessed promptly to exclude male breast cancer, which although rare does occur.

Will exercise get rid of gynaecomastia?

Exercise and weight loss reduce the fatty component, but true glandular tissue does not respond to exercise. That is why chest exercises often make no visible difference — and why gland excision is part of the operation where gland predominates.

What scars are left after gynaecomastia surgery?

Liposuction leaves a few millimetre-scale marks. Gland excision usually adds a fine scar along the lower edge of the areola, where it tends to fade well.

Is gynaecomastia surgery covered by insurance?

Usually not when it is cosmetic, though insurers may cover assessment of a new lump or suspicious change. Self-pay prices are confirmed in writing by the hospital before you commit.
