Bupa Open Referral: how to choose your breast surgeon

How Bupa Open Referral works for breast symptoms, what fee-assured means, and what to look for in a breast surgeon, explained by a consultant breast surgeon.

Mr Zaker Ullah FRCS13 January 2026 · 7 min read
Contents
How Bupa Open Referral worksWhat "fee-assured" means for youCan I ask for a specific breast surgeon?What to look for in a breast surgeonAfter you have chosenQuestions

In short: with a Bupa Open Referral, your GP names the specialty you need, such as breast surgery, rather than a particular surgeon. When you call Bupa, they offer you a choice of up to three recognised, fee-assured consultants. You can look each one up on Bupa Finder, and you choose who you see.

Many of my private patients at The Holly in Buckhurst Hill come to me through Bupa, and a good number of them arrive unsure how the system works. Some worry they have no say in who they see. Others have been given three names and have no idea how to pick between them. This post explains the process as Bupa describes it, and then what I would look for if I were choosing a breast surgeon for someone in my own family.

How Bupa Open Referral works

Bupa describes three ways an insured member can reach a specialist. A traditional named referral, where your GP writes to a particular consultant. Direct Access, which on some policies lets you call Bupa about certain symptoms, including suspected cancer, without seeing a GP first. And Open Referral, which is what this post is about.

With an Open Referral, the steps are usually these:

  1. Your GP assesses you and decides which type of specialist you need. For a breast lump, breast pain, nipple discharge or a skin change on the breast, that is a breast surgeon. The GP tells you the specialty rather than naming a surgeon.
  2. You call Bupa to pre-authorise the consultation. Have your membership number and the GP's advice to hand.
  3. Bupa offers you a choice of up to three consultants with the right expertise, who are recognised by Bupa and work in your area.
  4. You choose, often after looking at their profiles on Bupa Finder, Bupa's online directory of consultants.
  5. You book with your chosen consultant's secretary or hospital, quoting your pre-authorisation number.

Some Bupa policies are built entirely around Open Referral, which means you will be offered a shortlist rather than having a named consultant on your GP letter. Others allow either route. Your policy documents, or a call to Bupa, will tell you which you have. Your excess and benefit limits still apply in the usual way.

What "fee-assured" means for you

A fee-assured consultant has an agreement with Bupa to charge within the benefit limits your policy covers. In practice, if your care is covered by your policy, you should not receive an extra bill from the surgeon or anaesthetist for that care. Bupa's own explanation is that consultants offered through Open Referral are fee-assured, so this is one of the main attractions of the route.

The consultants Bupa offers through Open Referral also have to meet criteria Bupa sets on cost of care, clinical practice and patient experience. Bupa compares each consultant with others in the same specialty, looking at things such as complaints and how closely their practice follows national guidance.

Bupa also has a Platinum category. Platinum consultants are members of the Open Referral network, are fee-assured, and have treated a set minimum number of Bupa patients over three years. I am a Bupa Platinum consultant, fee-assured and in the Open Referral network, which means I can be offered to you through an Open Referral and you should not face a shortfall on my fees for covered care. I also see patients insured by AXA Health, Aviva, Vitality, WPA and other insurers, and my team can tell you how each one handles referrals.

Can I ask for a specific breast surgeon?

You can tell Bupa when you call if there is a consultant you would like to see. Whether they can offer that person depends on your policy and whether the consultant is in the right network for it. If your policy is an Open Referral one, the consultant needs to be in Bupa's Open Referral network. If you have a named referral from your GP to a consultant who is not covered on your policy, Bupa says it can offer you a choice of those who are.

The practical tip I give patients is simple. Look the surgeon up on Bupa Finder before you call. The profile shows whether they are fee-assured and whether they carry the Open Referral badge. If they do, mention them by name when you ring, and ask whether they can be included in your choice. Be honest with yourself about location and clinic days, too. A surgeon whose clinic you can reach easily is worth a great deal when you may need to come back for results or treatment.

What to look for in a breast surgeon

All three names on your shortlist will be recognised by Bupa and will meet Bupa's criteria. Choosing between them is about fit, not about finding a "winner". These are the questions I would ask.

Is breast surgery the main part of their practice? Many general surgeons once did some breast work. Today breast surgery is a specialist field, and you want someone whose regular work is assessing and treating breast problems, including breast cancer. The profile should make this clear.

Can they offer a one-stop clinic? For a new breast symptom, the UK standard is triple assessment: examination, imaging (ultrasound, mammogram or both) and a needle biopsy if anything needs sampling. At a one-stop breast clinic all three happen at the same visit, so you are not left waiting between separate appointments. Most people leave reassured, because most breast symptoms turn out to be benign.

Do they work within a multidisciplinary team? If a biopsy does show cancer, your plan should be agreed by a multidisciplinary team (MDT): surgeons, radiologists who read the scans, pathologists who examine the tissue, oncologists and specialist breast nurses. Ask whether the surgeon's private patients are discussed at an MDT. Mine are, before and after any breast cancer surgery.

Can they offer the full range of treatment if needed? If cancer is found, it helps to stay with the surgeon you already know. An oncoplastic breast surgeon can remove the cancer and plan the shape of the breast at the same time, and can discuss reconstruction where it is appropriate. It is worth knowing this from the start, even though for most people it will never be needed.

Are they on the GMC specialist register? Every doctor practising in the UK is listed on the General Medical Council's online register. You can search by name or GMC number and see whether they hold specialist registration, and in which specialty. My GMC number is 4368515, and I would encourage you to check any surgeon you are considering, including me.

Where and when do they see patients? Look at the hospitals they work at and the clinic days. I see private patients at three clinics. At Nuffield Health The Holly in Buckhurst Hill I run a Rapid Access Breast Clinic on Tuesday afternoons. I also consult at Bupa's Wharfside Health Centre in Canary Wharf and at Nuffield Health St Bartholomew's in the City.

If you have a new breast lump, a skin or nipple change, or a symptom that worries you, do not wait on paperwork. See your GP, or ask Bupa whether your policy includes Direct Access for suspected cancer. The NHS urgent referral route is also always open to you.

After you have chosen

Once you have picked a surgeon and have your pre-authorisation number, book through their secretary or the hospital. My booking page lists the ways to reach my team at each hospital. Bring a list of any medicines, the name of your GP, and any previous breast imaging reports if you have them. You can read what to expect on the day in my guide to the Rapid Access Breast Clinic at The Holly.

If you are not covered by insurance, or would rather not use it, you can book without a GP referral as a self-funding patient. I explain that route in seeing a private breast surgeon without a GP referral.

Questions

Does Bupa Open Referral mean I cannot choose my surgeon?

No. Bupa offers you a choice of up to three recognised consultants with the right expertise, and you decide which one to see. You can also ask whether a particular consultant can be included.

What does fee-assured mean with Bupa?

A fee-assured consultant charges within your policy's benefit limits, so you should not receive extra bills from them for treatment your policy covers. Your excess still applies.

Do I need a GP referral for a Bupa breast appointment?

Usually, yes, either a named or an open referral. Some policies include Direct Access for suspected cancer symptoms, which lets you call Bupa directly. Check your policy or ask Bupa.

How do I check a breast surgeon's qualifications?

Search the GMC's online register by name or GMC number to see their registration and any specialist registration. Bupa Finder also shows whether a consultant is fee-assured and in the Open Referral network.
