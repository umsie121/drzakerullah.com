**In short:** if you are paying for yourself, you can usually book a private consultation with a breast surgeon directly, with no GP referral. If you are using private medical insurance, most insurers ask for a GP referral before they authorise the appointment.

It is one of the questions my team is asked most often, so here is how it works in practice.

## Self-funding patients

Private hospitals in the UK accept self-referrals for outpatient consultations with most specialists, including breast surgeons. You call, book and attend. The hospital confirms the consultation fee in advance. If you need tests or surgery, you get a written quote before anything happens.

I still always write to your GP after the appointment, with your permission, so your NHS record stays complete. That matters for your long-term care.

## Insured patients

Most UK insurers, including Bupa, AXA Health, Aviva and Vitality, want two things before a specialist appointment:

1. **A GP referral.** Some insurers accept a referral from a digital GP service included in your policy. Some policies allow "open referral", meaning the GP recommends a specialty rather than a named surgeon.
2. **A pre-authorisation number.** Call your insurer with the referral and ask them to authorise an appointment with your chosen consultant.

Policies vary, so check yours before booking. I am fee-assured with the major insurers, so you should not face a shortfall for my fees. You can see where I consult on the [clinics page](~/locations/) and book through my [appointments page](~/booking/).

## What if I have a worrying symptom?

Don't let paperwork hold you up. If you have a new breast lump, skin change or nipple change, either:

- see your GP, who can refer you urgently on the NHS or privately, or
- book directly as a self-funding patient and sort out insurance later if needed.

A [one-stop breast clinic](~/procedures/benign-breast-conditions/) can usually give you an answer, most often a reassuring one, in a single visit.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a GP referral to see a breast surgeon privately?
Not if you are self-funding. Insured patients usually need one for their insurer to authorise the appointment.

### Will my GP be told about a private appointment?
With your consent, your consultant writes to your GP after the appointment so your medical record stays complete.

### Can I switch to the NHS after a private consultation?
Yes. You can move between private and NHS care. Your consultant or GP can refer you on to the NHS pathway if you need treatment there.
