**In short:** gynaecomastia gland excision means removing the firm disc of breast gland behind the nipple through a small cut at the edge of the areola. Liposuction removes fat well but often leaves this gland behind. Where gland makes up much of the swelling, removing it, usually alongside liposuction, gives a flatter and more lasting result.

Many of the men I see about gynaecomastia have already read a great deal online. A common question is whether liposuction on its own will do. Sometimes it will. Often, though, the firm tissue right behind the nipple is the part that bothers them most, and that tissue does not respond well to liposuction.

This post explains the difference between gland and fat, how I tell them apart in clinic, and what gland excision involves. It follows on from my earlier post on [a lump behind the nipple in men](~/news/lump-behind-nipple-men-gynaecomastia/), which covers how gynaecomastia is told apart from other causes.

## Gland or fat: what is actually making the chest bigger?

Gynaecomastia is a benign growth of breast gland tissue in men. It is common, and it has many causes: the hormonal changes of puberty and later life, weight gain, some medicines, anabolic steroids and, occasionally, a condition affecting the liver, kidneys or thyroid.

A fuller chest can be made up of two quite different tissues.

- **Gland.** A firm, rubbery disc centred under the nipple and areola (the darker skin around the nipple). It can be tender, especially when it first appears. It does not shrink with diet or exercise.
- **Fat.** Softer tissue spread more widely across the chest, often alongside weight elsewhere. It does reduce with weight loss. Enlargement made mainly of fat is sometimes called pseudogynaecomastia.

Most men have a mixture of the two. The balance between them is what decides the operation.

## How I tell the difference

The first step is always a proper assessment, not a discussion of surgery. I take a history, including medicines, supplements and any steroid use, and examine both sides of the chest and both armpits. A chaperone is always offered.

On examination, gland usually feels like a distinct firm plate under the nipple, while fat feels softer and blends into the surrounding chest. An ultrasound scan helps to confirm the balance. If anything about a lump is unusual, for example if it is hard, off to one side of the nipple or on one side only and growing, I arrange further imaging and sometimes a needle biopsy. Male breast cancer is uncommon, but it does occur, and I would always want to exclude it first. My post on [male breast cancer symptoms](~/news/male-breast-cancer-symptoms/) sets out the signs.

It is also worth treating any cause that can be treated. If a medicine is responsible, a safe alternative may help the tissue settle, and this should be discussed with the doctor who prescribed it. In teenagers, gynaecomastia often settles on its own within a year or two.

## When gynaecomastia gland excision is the right choice

Gland excision is usually part of the plan when:

- there is a firm disc of tissue directly behind the nipple;
- the nipple and areola look puffy or pushed forward, even when the rest of the chest is fairly flat;
- the gynaecomastia has been present for a long time, as long-standing gland tends to become firmer;
- it is related to past anabolic steroid use, where the tissue is often mostly gland;
- the swelling remains after a previous liposuction.

Liposuction alone may be enough when the enlargement is mostly fat and there is little firm tissue under the nipple. In practice, many men need both: liposuction to smooth and shape the chest, and gland excision to remove the central disc.

## What the operation involves

The operation is usually done as a day case under general anaesthetic.

1. **Liposuction**, where it is needed, is done first through a few small openings a few millimetres long. A thin tube removes fat and helps to blend the edges of the chest.
2. **Gland excision** is then done through a small curved incision along the lower edge of the areola. The firm tissue is removed from behind the nipple.
3. **Shaping.** A thin layer of tissue is deliberately left under the nipple. Removing too much can leave a dip, which is hard to correct later, so the aim is a flat, natural contour rather than a hollow.

Where there is a lot of loose skin, for example after major weight loss, further techniques may be needed to remove skin. These involve longer scars, and I explain them if they apply to you.

The removed gland tissue is usually sent to the laboratory to be examined under a microscope, as is routine for breast tissue.

## Risks, scars and recovery

Every operation carries risks, and I go through the ones that apply to you before you consent. For gynaecomastia surgery they include:

- **bleeding**, which occasionally collects under the skin (a haematoma) and may need a return to theatre;
- **fluid collecting** under the wound (a seroma);
- **infection**;
- **changes in nipple sensation**, which are often temporary;
- **unevenness or asymmetry**, or a contour irregularity;
- **scarring**, which along the areola edge usually fades well.

Afterwards you wear a compression vest for a few weeks. Most men are back at desk work within a week and return to full exercise at four to six weeks. The result is usually permanent, provided weight is stable and any medicine or steroid that caused the problem is avoided. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can liposuction alone fix gynaecomastia?
Sometimes, when the enlargement is mostly fat. When there is a firm disc of gland behind the nipple, liposuction usually leaves it behind, and gland excision is needed as well.

### Where is the scar after gynaecomastia gland excision?
Usually along the lower edge of the areola, where the change in skin colour helps it blend in. Liposuction adds a few small marks a few millimetres long.

### Will exercise get rid of gynaecomastia gland tissue?
No. Exercise and weight loss reduce fat, but true gland tissue does not respond to them. This is why chest exercises often make little difference.

### Can gynaecomastia come back after surgery?
The result is usually permanent. It can recur if a medicine or anabolic steroid that caused it is taken again, or with significant weight gain.
