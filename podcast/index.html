In this episode

The five questions

Lumpectomy or mastectomy: how is the decision made?

It depends on the size of the cancer relative to the breast, where it lies, whether there is more than one area, genetics and, above all, the patient’s own wishes. Oncoplastic techniques mean many women who once needed a mastectomy can keep their breast. Breast cancer surgery.

What symptoms should I look for, and how should I check?

Not only a lump: dimpling of the skin, a change in size or shape, a newly inverted nipple, a rash on the nipple or bloodstained discharge, and persistent pain in one place. Know what is normal for you and have any change checked. When to see a specialist.

Is breast cancer rising in young women?

UK figures show breast cancer has become more common in younger women in recent decades, although it remains far less common under 40. No woman should be told she is too young to have a new symptom assessed.

Does everyone need radiotherapy?

No. Radiotherapy is standard after most breast conserving surgery and is used selectively after mastectomy. Some patients at lower risk can safely avoid it. The decision is made by the multidisciplinary team, based on the biology and stage of the cancer.

What is triple negative breast cancer?

A type of breast cancer without oestrogen, progesterone or HER2 receptors, so hormone and HER2 treatments do not work against it. It is often treated with chemotherapy, sometimes with immunotherapy, before surgery, and many patients respond well to modern treatment.
