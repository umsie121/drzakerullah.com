Fat grafting to the breast

Fat grafting — lipofilling — moves a small amount of your own fat, harvested by gentle liposuction, into the breast to smooth contours, fill defects and soften scarred tissue.

Book a consultation FAQs
Written and medically reviewed by Mr Md Zaker Ullah FRCS, Consultant Oncoplastic Breast Surgeon · Updated 3 November 2026

What it is used for

In breast surgery, fat grafting is chiefly a refining tool: filling a dent left by breast-conserving cancer surgery, smoothing the edges of an implant reconstruction, softening tissue damaged by radiotherapy and correcting small asymmetries. It uses no foreign material and leaves only tiny marks at the donor and recipient sites.

How it works

Fat is harvested by gentle liposuction — usually from the abdomen or thighs — purified, and injected into the breast in fine threads so that each droplet gains a blood supply. A proportion of grafted fat (commonly 30–50%) is naturally resorbed in the first months, which is why larger corrections are often staged over two or three sessions a few months apart.

Recovery

Fat grafting is usually a day-case procedure. Bruising at the donor site is the main after-effect; most patients are back to normal activities within days. The final result is judged at around three months, once swelling has settled.

This page is general information, not medical advice. Whether a procedure is right for you can only be decided in consultation, after proper assessment.

FAQ

Common questions

Is fat grafting safe after breast cancer?

Current evidence has not shown lipofilling to increase the risk of cancer recurrence, and it is widely used in reconstructive practice. Grafted fat can cause benign changes on mammograms (oil cysts, calcification) which specialists recognise; your imaging team should always know you have had lipofilling.

How many sessions will I need?

Small refinements are often achieved in one session. Larger corrections typically need two or three sessions, spaced roughly three months apart, because a proportion of each graft is resorbed.

Does the transferred fat behave like normal fat?

Yes — once it has taken, grafted fat is living tissue. It feels natural and will gain and lose volume with your weight like fat anywhere else.
