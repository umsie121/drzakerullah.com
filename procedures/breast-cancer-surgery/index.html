Breast cancer

Breast cancer surgery

Surgery is usually the foundation of breast cancer treatment. As an oncoplastic surgeon, Mr Ullah plans the removal of the cancer and the appearance of the breast together, before the operation, so neither is an afterthought.

Breast conserving surgery

Where the size and position of the tumour allow, the preferred operation is usually a wide local excision, or lumpectomy. The cancer is removed with a margin of healthy tissue and the breast is kept. Oncoplastic techniques allow Mr Ullah to remove a larger area than conventional surgery would permit, then reshape the remaining tissue so the breast keeps a natural form.

In women with larger breasts this can be a therapeutic mammoplasty, in which the cancer is removed using the techniques of a breast reduction. The result is a lighter, lifted breast, and the other breast can be matched at the same operation or later.

Mastectomy

Some cancers, and some patients' own preferences, are better served by mastectomy. Where appropriate it can be skin sparing or nipple sparing, with immediate reconstruction, so that the shape of the breast is restored before you wake. Mr Ullah discusses reconstruction before mastectomy, not after it.

The lymph nodes

Assessing the armpit (axilla) is part of almost every breast cancer operation. For most patients this means a sentinel lymph node biopsy: only the first one to three nodes draining the breast are removed, located with a tracer. It gives the oncologists the information they need and greatly reduces the risk of arm swelling (lymphoedema). After chemotherapy, a targeted axillary dissection can remove the specific nodes that were affected, and where needed an axillary clearance is performed.

DCIS and risk reducing surgery

DCIS (ductal carcinoma in situ) is an early, non-invasive change that is usually treated with breast conserving surgery. For women who carry a BRCA or other high-risk gene variant, Mr Ullah offers risk reducing mastectomy with reconstruction, after careful counselling.

A team decision

Every cancer case is discussed by a multidisciplinary team of radiologists, pathologists, oncologists and specialist nurses, before and after surgery. Chemotherapy, radiotherapy and hormone treatment are planned around the operation so that each part of treatment supports the others.

Questions

Questions about breast cancer surgery

How soon after diagnosis will I have surgery?

Once the multidisciplinary team has agreed the plan, private surgery can usually be arranged within one to two weeks, depending on the operation and any tests still needed. Some patients have chemotherapy first. When that happens it is a deliberate part of the plan, not a delay.

Will I lose my breast?

Most breast cancers are now treated with breast conserving surgery, and oncoplastic techniques have widened who is suitable. Where mastectomy is the safer choice, immediate reconstruction is usually possible. The decision is always made with you.

What is oncoplastic breast surgery?

Oncoplastic surgery combines complete removal of the cancer with plastic surgical techniques that preserve or restore the shape of the breast, at the same operation. Cancer control is at least as good as with standard surgery. Read Mr Ullah's article.

What are the risks of breast cancer surgery?

As with any operation, there are risks of bleeding, infection, fluid collection (seroma) and scarring, along with risks particular to each procedure, such as altered sensation or, after surgery to the armpit, lymphoedema. Mr Ullah explains the risks of your operation in detail before you consent.

Can I have a second opinion before surgery?

Yes, and it is your right to ask. Many patients see Mr Ullah for a second opinion on a diagnosis or a proposed operation. More about second opinions.
