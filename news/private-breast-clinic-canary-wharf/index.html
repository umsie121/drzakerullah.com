Private breast clinic in Canary Wharf: a breast surgeon near work

A private breast clinic in Canary Wharf, explained by breast surgeon Mr Zaker Ullah: getting to Bank Street, employer insurance, self-funding and tests.

By Mr Md Zaker Ullah FRCS, Consultant Oncoplastic Breast Surgeon.

In short: I see private patients at Bupa Wharfside Health Centre, 50 Bank Street, a few minutes' walk from Canary Wharf station. You can come with employer health insurance, usually after a GP or open referral and a pre-authorisation number, or pay for yourself. Any tests or surgery are arranged at The Holly or Nuffield Health St Bartholomew's.

Many of the people I see in Canary Wharf work on the Canary Wharf estate itself. Some live nearby, in Docklands, the Isle of Dogs, Wapping or Limehouse, and others come in from Greenwich, Stratford, Bow or the City. What they have in common is a busy working day and, often, a health insurance policy through their employer that they have never had to use before.

Finding a breast change is worrying enough without having to work out how private care fits around meetings and commutes. This post sets out the practical side: how to reach the clinic, how employer insurance works, what happens at the first appointment, and how tests and any treatment are organised afterwards.

Getting to the clinic on Bank Street

Bupa Wharfside Health Centre is at 50 Bank Street, London E14 5NT, in the middle of the Canary Wharf estate.

  • Jubilee line or Elizabeth line. Canary Wharf station serves both, and the clinic is a few minutes' walk away.
  • DLR. The clinic is close to the DLR, which is often the quickest route from Greenwich, Limehouse, Bow or Stratford.

The clinic's own number is 020 4604 9300. The address, a map and more about the centre are on my Canary Wharf clinic page.

Using employer health insurance at a private breast clinic in Canary Wharf

A lot of my Canary Wharf patients are covered by a company scheme. These usually work in the same way as a personal policy, but it helps to know the steps before you book.

  1. Get a referral. Most insurers want a GP referral letter before the first appointment. This can be from your own NHS GP or, where your scheme offers one, a private or virtual GP service.
  2. Call your insurer. Tell them you have a breast symptom and would like to see a breast surgeon. They will check your cover and give you a pre-authorisation number.
  3. Book the appointment and bring the number with you.

Some company schemes have their own rules about referrals or which hospitals you can use, so it is worth asking the helpline exactly what your policy covers before the first visit.

If you are with Bupa

I am a Bupa Platinum consultant and fee-assured, so you should not face a shortfall on my fees for treatment your policy covers. I am also part of Bupa's Open Referral network. An open referral names the specialty rather than a particular surgeon, and Bupa then suggests consultants. If you would like to see me, you can ask for me by name. I have written more about how this works in Bupa Open Referral and choosing a breast surgeon.

I am also recognised by other major insurers, including AXA Health, Aviva, Vitality, WPA and Cigna. The details are on my fees and insurance page.

If you are paying for yourself

You do not need insurance or a GP referral. The consultation fee is confirmed when you book. If you need tests or surgery, you receive a written, fixed price before anything is arranged, and nothing goes ahead without your agreement. With your permission, I write to your GP afterwards so your NHS record stays complete.

Questions about your own situation? A consultation with Mr Ullah can often be arranged within days.

What the first consultation covers

The first appointment is about understanding what you have noticed and deciding, together, what needs to happen next.

  • We talk first. I ask about the symptom, how long it has been there, whether it changes with your menstrual cycle, any previous breast problems, your medicines and your family history. I also ask what is worrying you most.
  • I examine both breasts and both armpits. A chaperone is always offered.
  • We agree a plan. For many symptoms that means imaging. Depending on your age and what I find, that is an ultrasound scan, a mammogram (a low dose breast X-ray) or both, and sometimes a needle biopsy, where a small sample of tissue is taken under local anaesthetic.

Examination, imaging and biopsy together are called triple assessment. It is the standard way of assessing any new breast symptom in the UK, and it is how I approach breast lumps, breast pain and other benign breast conditions. Most breast symptoms turn out to be benign, but the aim is a clear answer rather than a guess.

I consult in English, Bangla and Arabic, which some patients and families find makes the conversation easier.

How tests and any surgery are arranged

The Wharfside clinic is where we meet and talk. If you need scans or a biopsy, I arrange them at the hospital that suits you:

  • Nuffield Health St Bartholomew's in Smithfield, EC1. It is easy to reach from Canary Wharf on the Elizabeth line, and a few minutes on foot from Farringdon. It offers consultations, imaging and day case surgery. There is more on my City of London clinic page.
  • Nuffield Health The Holly in Buckhurst Hill, IG9, on the London and Essex border. It has imaging and full operating theatres, and parking on site. It often suits people who live in east London or Essex and would rather be treated near home than near work.

You choose. Some patients want everything close to the office; others prefer to have a procedure near home, where it is easier to rest afterwards.

If treatment is needed, you stay with me. That might be removing a benign lump such as a fibroadenoma, or, if a biopsy shows cancer, breast cancer surgery. Every cancer case is discussed by a multidisciplinary team, the radiologists, pathologists, oncologists and specialist nurses who plan treatment together, and the operation is then carried out at The Holly or St Bartholomew's, whichever is right for you and for the operation.

Fitting appointments around work

I know that for many people the hardest part is not the appointment itself but finding the time. A few things help.

  • Say it is a new symptom when you book. New breast symptoms are given priority.
  • Ask about timing. My secretary can tell you which appointments are available and help you plan around fixed commitments.
  • Use video where it makes sense. A first conversation, a discussion of results or some follow-up appointments can be done by video or telephone. If you need an examination or tests, we arrange a face to face appointment. More about this is on my video consultations page.
  • You do not have to tell your employer the details. Your insurer needs clinical information to authorise care, but your manager does not. Medical information is shared only with your consent.

All the ways to book, including my private secretary, Doctify, Top Doctors and Bupa Finder, are on my booking page.

What to bring: your referral letter and pre-authorisation number if you are insured, any previous mammogram or ultrasound reports, and a list of your medicines. If you might need a mammogram, it helps to avoid deodorant or talcum powder on the day.

Should you go private at all?

I lead the NHS breast unit at Whipps Cross, and I would never want anyone to feel they must pay to be seen. If your GP thinks your symptom could be cancer, the NHS will see you on an urgent suspected cancer pathway. I have set out the honest differences between the two routes in the two-week wait versus a private breast clinic. For many people with employer insurance, the private route simply gives more choice over where and when they are seen.

If you notice a new lump, a change in the skin of the breast, or a change in the nipple, please do not wait for a convenient gap in your diary. See your GP or book promptly. The NHS has a clear guide to breast cancer symptoms if you want to check what to look out for.

Questions

Where is the private breast clinic in Canary Wharf?

I see patients at Bupa Wharfside Health Centre, 50 Bank Street, London E14 5NT. It is a few minutes' walk from Canary Wharf station on the Jubilee and Elizabeth lines, and close to the DLR.

Can I use my employer's health insurance to see a breast surgeon?

Usually, yes. Most schemes ask for a GP referral and a pre-authorisation number before the first appointment, so call your insurer's helpline first and check what your policy covers.

Are scans done at the Canary Wharf clinic?

I arrange any scans or biopsy at the hospital that suits you, either Nuffield Health St Bartholomew's in the City or Nuffield Health The Holly in Buckhurst Hill. We agree which suits you at your consultation.

Do I need a GP referral if I am paying for myself?

No. Self-funding patients can book directly. The consultation fee is confirmed when you book, and any tests or treatment are priced in writing before they go ahead.
