Appointments

How long is the first appointment?

Allow up to an hour and a half if you have a new breast symptom, so that imaging and any biopsy can take place at the same visit. Follow-up appointments are shorter.

Can I bring someone with me?

Yes, and many patients do. A relative or friend can help you remember what was said and ask the questions you may forget.

Will my GP be told?

With your consent, Mr Ullah writes to your GP after each consultation so that your medical record stays complete.

Can I have a chaperone?

Yes. A chaperone is always available for examinations, and you can ask for one at any point.

Fees and insurance

Do I need a GP referral if I am insured?

Most insurers ask for a GP referral before they authorise a specialist appointment. Some accept a referral from a digital GP service included in your policy. Check with your insurer.

Is Mr Ullah fee-assured?

Yes. Mr Ullah is a Bupa Platinum consultant and fee-assured with Bupa, and is recognised by the other major UK insurers, so you should not face a shortfall for his fees. Confirm your own cover with your insurer.

How much does a consultation cost if I am self-funding?

Consultation fees are confirmed by the hospital or booking service when you book, before you attend. Imaging and biopsies are usually charged separately, and for tests or surgery you receive a written, fixed price before you commit.

Is breast reconstruction covered?

Reconstruction after breast cancer, including surgery to the other breast for symmetry, is normally covered by insurers as part of cancer treatment. Cosmetic procedures generally are not.

Breast lumps and pain

Is a breast lump usually cancer?

No. The large majority of breast lumps are benign: cysts, fibroadenomas or normal nodular tissue. There is no reliable way to tell them apart by touch, which is why every new lump should be examined, imaged and, if needed, biopsied.

What is triple assessment?

Triple assessment is clinical examination, imaging (mammogram, ultrasound or both) and a needle biopsy where indicated. When all three agree, the diagnosis is highly reliable. A one-stop clinic provides all three at one visit.

How quickly can I be seen privately?

New breast symptoms are given priority, and an appointment can often be arranged within days. Call the private secretary or send an enquiry.

Should I worry about breast pain?

Breast pain on its own is very rarely a sign of cancer and is one of the most common reasons women see a breast specialist. Pain that persists in one particular spot should still be assessed. A clear diagnosis is often the most effective treatment.

Do I need a GP referral?

Insured patients usually need a GP referral before their insurer will authorise the appointment. Self-funding patients can book directly.

Breast cancer surgery

How soon after diagnosis will I have surgery?

Once the multidisciplinary team has agreed the plan, private surgery can usually be arranged within one to two weeks, depending on the operation and any tests still needed. Some patients have chemotherapy first. When that happens it is a deliberate part of the plan, not a delay.

Will I lose my breast?

Most breast cancers are now treated with breast conserving surgery, and oncoplastic techniques have widened who is suitable. Where mastectomy is the safer choice, immediate reconstruction is usually possible. The decision is always made with you.

What is oncoplastic breast surgery?

Oncoplastic surgery combines complete removal of the cancer with plastic surgical techniques that preserve or restore the shape of the breast, at the same operation. Cancer control is at least as good as with standard surgery. Read Mr Ullah's article.

What are the risks of breast cancer surgery?

As with any operation, there are risks of bleeding, infection, fluid collection (seroma) and scarring, along with risks particular to each procedure, such as altered sensation or, after surgery to the armpit, lymphoedema. Mr Ullah explains the risks of your operation in detail before you consent.

Can I have a second opinion before surgery?

Yes, and it is your right to ask. Many patients see Mr Ullah for a second opinion on a diagnosis or a proposed operation. More about second opinions.

Breast reconstruction

Will a reconstructed breast look and feel natural?

Reconstruction restores shape and symmetry in clothes very reliably, and modern techniques can look natural without clothes too. Sensation is usually reduced. Reconstructions made from your own tissue generally feel more natural than implants.

Is reconstruction safe if I need radiotherapy?

Radiotherapy increases the rate of complications for some types of reconstruction, implants in particular. If radiotherapy is likely, it shapes the recommended technique or timing, which is why reconstruction is planned alongside cancer treatment.

Can I have reconstruction years after my mastectomy?

Yes. Delayed reconstruction is routine and there is no time limit. Women treated ten or more years ago still have options, depending on their health and previous treatment.

Is breast reconstruction covered by insurance?

Reconstruction after breast cancer, including surgery to the other breast for symmetry, is normally covered by UK private medical insurers as part of cancer treatment. Confirm with your insurer beforehand.

Breast reduction

Will breast reduction affect nipple sensation or breastfeeding?

Both can be affected. Some change in nipple sensation is common at first and usually improves, although it can be permanent. Many women can breastfeed after a reduction, but it cannot be guaranteed. If future breastfeeding matters to you, say so, because it influences technique and timing.

What scars does a breast reduction leave?

Usually a scar around the areola, a vertical line down to the fold beneath the breast, and often a line within the fold itself, known as the anchor pattern. Scars are red and firm at first and fade considerably over 12 to 24 months.

Is breast reduction covered by insurance?

Cosmetic reduction generally is not. Symmetrisation after breast cancer surgery is usually treated as part of reconstruction. Your insurer will confirm cover in advance.

How long is recovery after breast reduction?

Most women are back at desk work within one to two weeks, driving at around two weeks, and exercising fully at about six weeks. A supportive bra is worn day and night for several weeks.

Fat grafting

Is fat grafting safe after breast cancer?

Current evidence has not shown that fat grafting increases the risk of cancer returning, and it is widely used in reconstruction. Grafted fat can produce benign changes on mammograms, such as oil cysts or calcification, which specialists recognise. Always tell your imaging team that you have had fat grafting.

How many sessions will I need?

Small refinements often need a single session. Larger corrections usually need two or three sessions about three months apart, because some of each graft is absorbed.

Does the transferred fat behave like normal fat?

Yes. Once established, grafted fat is living tissue. It feels natural and will gain and lose volume with your weight, like fat elsewhere.

Gynaecomastia

Is gynaecomastia dangerous?

Almost never. It is a benign enlargement of breast gland tissue. A firm lump on one side, a lump that is growing, or discharge from the nipple should be assessed promptly to exclude male breast cancer, which is rare but does occur.

Will exercise get rid of gynaecomastia?

Exercise and weight loss reduce the fatty part, but true gland tissue does not respond to exercise. This is why chest exercises often make no visible difference, and why removing the gland is part of the operation when gland predominates.

What scars are left after gynaecomastia surgery?

Liposuction leaves a few marks a few millimetres long. Removing the gland usually adds a fine scar along the lower edge of the areola, where it tends to fade well.

Is gynaecomastia surgery covered by insurance?

Usually not when it is cosmetic, although insurers may cover assessment of a new lump or a suspicious change. The hospital confirms self-pay prices in writing before you commit.

Hernia and gallbladder

What are the advantages of keyhole surgery?

Smaller incisions mean less pain, a shorter stay in hospital, a faster return to work and smaller scars. Not every patient or condition is suitable, and a small proportion of keyhole operations are converted to open surgery for safety. That possibility is always discussed before consent.

How long does hernia repair recovery take?

After keyhole repair most people return to desk work within one to two weeks and to heavy lifting at four to six weeks. Recovery after open repair takes a little longer. You may drive once you can make an emergency stop comfortably.

Do gallstones always need surgery?

No. Gallstones found by chance that cause no symptoms can usually be left alone. Surgery is advised once they cause pain or complications, because further attacks are then likely.

Lumps, cysts and moles

Is a lipoma dangerous?

Lipomas are benign, and many can be left alone. They are usually removed when they are growing, uncomfortable or unwanted. A lump that is large, deep, painful or growing quickly should be scanned first to be sure of the diagnosis.

Will removing a sebaceous cyst leave a scar?

There is always a small scar, usually a fine line a little longer than the cyst. Removing the whole cyst wall reduces the chance of it coming back.

Should a changing mole be checked?

Yes. A mole that is changing in size, shape or colour, has an irregular edge, bleeds or itches should be seen promptly. Most are harmless, but a mole of concern is removed and examined in the laboratory.

Is minor skin surgery covered by insurance?

Insurers usually cover the removal of lesions that are suspicious or causing symptoms, but not purely cosmetic removal. Self-funding patients receive a fixed price from the hospital in advance.
