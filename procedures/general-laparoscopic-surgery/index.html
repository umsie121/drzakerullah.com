Gallstones and gallbladder removal

Gallstones that cause pain, inflammation or pancreatitis are best treated by removing the gallbladder, an operation called laparoscopic cholecystectomy. It is performed through four small incisions and is one of the most common operations in the United Kingdom. Most patients go home the same day or after one night and return to normal activity within a fortnight. Life without a gallbladder is entirely normal.

Hernia repair

Mr Ullah repairs all types of hernia. A hernia in the groin (inguinal), at the navel (umbilical) or through a previous scar (incisional) will not repair itself. Mr Ullah repairs hernias by keyhole and by open surgery, usually reinforced with mesh, choosing the approach that suits the hernia and the patient. His keyhole hernia technique won the prize for best video presentation at the European Association for Endoscopic Surgery in 2003.

Appendicectomy

Suspected appendicitis needs urgent assessment. When surgery is required, a laparoscopic appendicectomy both confirms the diagnosis and treats it through small incisions, with a faster recovery than open surgery.

Questions

Questions about hernia and gallbladder

What are the advantages of keyhole surgery?

Smaller incisions mean less pain, a shorter stay in hospital, a faster return to work and smaller scars. Not every patient or condition is suitable, and a small proportion of keyhole operations are converted to open surgery for safety. That possibility is always discussed before consent.

How long does hernia repair recovery take?

After keyhole repair most people return to desk work within one to two weeks and to heavy lifting at four to six weeks. Recovery after open repair takes a little longer. You may drive once you can make an emergency stop comfortably.

Do gallstones always need surgery?

No. Gallstones found by chance that cause no symptoms can usually be left alone. Surgery is advised once they cause pain or complications, because further attacks are then likely.
