**In short:** the NHS "two week wait" breast clinic is the urgent referral your GP makes for a breast symptom. You have a full triple assessment and should know within 28 days whether cancer has been found or ruled out. A private one-stop clinic offers the same tests, with a choice of time and consultant. Both are good routes.

I work on both sides of this question. I lead the NHS breast unit at Whipps Cross University Hospital, where I introduced our one-stop breast clinic, and I run a private Rapid Access Breast Clinic at The Holly in Buckhurst Hill. Patients often ask me, a little apologetically, whether they should "go private" for a lump. My honest answer is that the medicine is the same. What differs is the practical side, and that matters more to some people than to others.

## How the NHS two week wait breast clinic works

When you see your GP with a breast symptom, they examine you and decide how to refer you. NICE guidance (NG12) tells GPs to use the urgent suspected cancer pathway for, among other things:

- anyone aged 30 or over with an unexplained breast lump, with or without pain
- anyone aged 50 or over with discharge, retraction or another change of concern in one nipple only

GPs can also refer urgently for skin changes that look suspicious, or for an unexplained lump in the armpit. Younger women with a lump may be referred less urgently, because cancer is much less likely under 30, although every lump still gets looked at properly.

For years this route was known as the "two week wait", because the target was a first hospital appointment within two weeks of referral. Most people, GPs included, still call it that. In October 2023 NHS England replaced that target with the **Faster Diagnosis Standard**. The aim now is that you are told whether you have cancer, or that cancer has been ruled out, within 28 days of the referral. The urgent referral itself has not changed. The measure simply moved from "seen" to "answered", which I think is the more useful thing for a patient.

Breast referrals where the GP does not suspect cancer are covered by the same 28 day standard. [Cancer Research UK explains the current waiting time standards](https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/about-cancer/worried-about-cancer/cancer-waiting-times) clearly if you want the detail. It also makes a point I repeat in clinic every week: the large majority of people referred urgently do not have cancer.

## What happens at the clinic, NHS or private

The assessment is called **triple assessment**, and it is the standard of care for breast symptoms across the UK:

1. **Examination.** A breast specialist takes a history, asks about family history and medicines, and examines both breasts and both armpits.
2. **Imaging.** An ultrasound scan, a mammogram or both, chosen by your age and your symptom. Under about 40, ultrasound is usually the first test, because younger breast tissue is dense and shows less clearly on a mammogram.
3. **Needle biopsy if needed.** If anything on the scan needs a closer look, a small sample is taken under local anaesthetic, guided by ultrasound.

When the three parts agree, the diagnosis is very reliable. In most NHS breast units, and at my private clinics, all three can happen at the same visit. That is what "one-stop" means. Biopsy results take a few working days wherever you are seen, because the tissue has to be processed and read by a pathologist.

I want to be clear about this part, because it is the part people worry about. The radiologists, the scanning machines and the multidisciplinary team meetings that review every cancer diagnosis work to the same national standards in both settings. If you are referred to an NHS breast clinic, you are in good hands.

## What a private one-stop breast clinic adds

Going private does not buy you better tests. It buys you choices, and for some people those choices are worth a great deal.

- **Choosing when.** You book an appointment that fits around work, school runs or caring for someone. The NHS has to offer the next available slot.
- **Choosing who.** You see the consultant you chose, by name, rather than whichever member of the team is running clinic that day. In the NHS you will be seen by a skilled specialist, but it may be a different person at each visit.
- **Continuity.** At my private clinics, the surgeon who examines you is the surgeon who gives your results and, if needed, does your operation. Some patients find that steadying, particularly if the news is not straightforward.
- **Time.** Private appointments usually allow longer for questions, and a partner or relative is welcome to come in with you.
- **Where.** You pick the hospital. Others prefer [Canary Wharf](~/locations/canary-wharf/) or the City.

Private care does come with costs. If you are self-funding, the hospital confirms the consultation fee when you book and gives you a written quote before any tests or surgery. If you have insurance, check your cover and get a pre-authorisation number first. ## How to choose between them

There is no wrong answer here. A few practical thoughts from my clinics:

- **Don't wait while you decide.** If you have found a new lump or a change in the nipple or skin, book to see your GP now. They can refer you urgently on the NHS, or write a private referral, or both.
- **You don't always need a GP letter to go private.** Self-funding patients can book directly. Insured patients usually need a referral for their insurer. I have written more about [seeing a breast surgeon without a GP referral](~/news/private-breast-surgeon-without-gp-referral/).
- **You can move between the two.** If you are seen privately and need cancer treatment, you can choose to have it on the NHS, and your consultant will refer you. Your GP is kept informed either way.
- **Think about what would help you most.** If you can be seen promptly on the NHS and are happy with that, it is an excellent route. If a choice of time, a named consultant or the same surgeon throughout would ease your mind, private care may suit you better.

:::note
Whichever route you take, please don't sit on a new breast symptom. Most turn out to be benign, such as cysts, fibroadenomas or normal lumpy tissue, but the only way to be sure is a proper assessment. More on [breast lumps, pain and benign breast conditions](~/procedures/benign-breast-conditions/).
:::

## Frequently asked questions

### Does the two week wait for breast symptoms still exist?
The two week target was replaced in England in October 2023 by the 28 day Faster Diagnosis Standard. GPs still make urgent suspected cancer referrals for breast symptoms in the same way, and many people still call it the two week wait.

### Is a private breast clinic better than the NHS?
The tests and the standards are the same. A private clinic offers a choice of appointment time, a named consultant and continuity with one surgeon, which some patients value. The quality of the diagnosis does not depend on the route.

### Does an urgent breast referral mean my doctor thinks I have cancer?
No. It means the symptom fits the national criteria for a fast assessment. Most people referred urgently are found not to have cancer.

### Can I be referred on the NHS and also see someone privately?
Yes. Some patients keep their NHS appointment while also booking privately. If you do, tell both teams, so your results and records stay joined up.
