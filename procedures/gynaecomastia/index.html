Men's and general surgery

Gynaecomastia, or male breast reduction

Enlargement of the male breast is common, benign in nearly every case, and treatable. It still deserves a specialist assessment, both to exclude the rare serious cause and to achieve a result worth having.

Mr Zaker Ullah FRCSConsultant Oncoplastic Breast Surgeon · Updated 4 November 2026

First, a diagnosis

Gynaecomastia has many causes: hormonal change in adolescence and later life, weight, some medicines and, occasionally, an underlying medical condition. Male breast cancer is rare but does occur. For that reason assessment by a breast specialist, with imaging and biopsy where needed, comes before any discussion of surgery. Gynaecomastia in teenagers often settles without treatment, and some settles once the underlying cause is treated.

Surgery

Where the tissue is established and troubling, surgery removes it. Depending on the balance of gland and fat, Mr Ullah uses liposuction, removal of the gland through a small incision at the edge of the areola, or both. Where there is significant excess skin, further techniques may be needed, and he will explain them if they apply to you.

Recovery and results

The operation is usually a day case under general anaesthetic. A compression garment is worn for a few weeks. Most men return to desk work within a week and to full exercise at four to six weeks. The result is usually permanent, provided weight and any responsible medicine are managed.

This page is general information and not medical advice. Whether a procedure is right for you can only be decided in consultation.

Questions

Questions about gynaecomastia

Is gynaecomastia dangerous?

Almost never. It is a benign enlargement of breast gland tissue. A firm lump on one side, a lump that is growing, or discharge from the nipple should be assessed promptly to exclude male breast cancer, which is rare but does occur.

Will exercise get rid of gynaecomastia?

Exercise and weight loss reduce the fatty part, but true gland tissue does not respond to exercise. This is why chest exercises often make no visible difference, and why removing the gland is part of the operation when gland predominates.

What scars are left after gynaecomastia surgery?

Liposuction leaves a few marks a few millimetres long. Removing the gland usually adds a fine scar along the lower edge of the areola, where it tends to fade well.

Is gynaecomastia surgery covered by insurance?

Usually not when it is cosmetic, although insurers may cover assessment of a new lump or a suspicious change. The hospital confirms self-pay prices in writing before you commit.
