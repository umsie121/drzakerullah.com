Treatment 01

Breast lumps, pain and benign breast conditions

Most breast symptoms are benign. Every one of them still deserves a clear answer, and quickly. Mr Ullah introduced the one-stop breast clinic at Whipps Cross and runs his private clinics in the same way.

Symptoms Mr Ullah assesses

  • A new lump or area of thickening in the breast or armpit
  • Breast pain (mastalgia), whether cyclical, constant or in one place
  • Nipple discharge, a newly inverted nipple or a rash on the nipple
  • Dimpling, puckering or redness of the skin
  • Breast cysts and fibroadenomas
  • A change in the size or shape of one breast
  • A family history of breast cancer, and questions about personal risk

The one-stop approach

The traditional route to a diagnosis is a clinic appointment, then a separate scan, then a further appointment for results. It can stretch an anxious wait across several weeks. At a one-stop clinic Mr Ullah takes a full history and examines you, arranges ultrasound or mammography at the same visit according to your age and symptoms, and takes a needle biopsy there and then if anything needs sampling.

This is triple assessment, the standard of care for breast symptoms in the United Kingdom. Most patients leave the first appointment either reassured or with a clear plan.

If treatment is needed

Many benign conditions need no treatment once the diagnosis is certain. Where treatment helps, Mr Ullah offers drainage of cysts, removal of symptomatic fibroadenomas and other benign lumps, duct surgery for troublesome nipple discharge, and structured advice for breast pain.

Family history and breast cancer risk

If close relatives have had breast or ovarian cancer, Mr Ullah offers a personal risk assessment, with advice on screening, genetic testing and, for some women, risk reducing options. Most people with a family history are at only moderately raised risk, and a clear assessment is reassuring in itself.

If a biopsy shows cancer, you are already in the care of an experienced oncoplastic breast surgeon, and your treatment continues with the same surgeon.

Questions

Questions about breast lumps and pain

Is a breast lump usually cancer?

No. The large majority of breast lumps are benign: cysts, fibroadenomas or normal nodular tissue. There is no reliable way to tell them apart by touch, which is why every new lump should be examined, imaged and, if needed, biopsied.

What is triple assessment?

Triple assessment is clinical examination, imaging (mammogram, ultrasound or both) and a needle biopsy where indicated. When all three agree, the diagnosis is highly reliable. A one-stop clinic provides all three at one visit.

How quickly can I be seen privately?

New breast symptoms are given priority, and an appointment can often be arranged within days. Call the private secretary or send an enquiry.

Should I worry about breast pain?

Breast pain on its own is very rarely a sign of cancer and is one of the most common reasons women see a breast specialist. Pain that persists in one particular spot should still be assessed. A clear diagnosis is often the most effective treatment.

Do I need a GP referral?

Insured patients usually need a GP referral before their insurer will authorise the appointment. Self-funding patients can book directly.
