**In short:** keyhole and open repair both work well for most groin (inguinal) hernias, and NICE recommends keyhole surgery as one of the options. Keyhole repair often means a quicker early recovery and suits hernias on both sides or ones that have come back. Open repair can be done under local anaesthetic and suits some larger hernias.

A groin hernia is one of the most common reasons people come to see me on the general surgical side of my practice. Usually someone has noticed a bulge in the groin that comes and goes, often when they stand, cough or lift. By the time they reach me, they have usually read about two kinds of operation and want to know which is better.

The honest answer is that neither is better for everyone. Both are well established, both usually use a mesh to strengthen the weak area, and both give good results in experienced hands. What I try to do is match the approach to the hernia and to the person. I have a long interest in this: my keyhole hernia technique, using a glove balloon, won the prize for best video presentation at the European Association for Endoscopic Surgery in 2003.

## What is an inguinal hernia?

An inguinal hernia happens when fatty tissue or part of the bowel pushes through a weak spot in the muscle wall of the lower abdomen, in the groin. It shows as a lump that may disappear when you lie down. It is much more common in men than in women.

A hernia does not heal by itself. That does not mean everyone needs an operation straight away. The NHS advises that a hernia that is not causing symptoms may not need treatment, and for some people, particularly those with a small hernia and few symptoms, keeping an eye on it is a reasonable choice. Surgery is usually recommended when a hernia causes pain or discomfort, gets bigger, or gets in the way of work or exercise.

:::note
Go to A&E straight away if a hernia becomes suddenly painful, firm or tender and will not go back in, or if you are being sick or cannot pass wind or open your bowels. These can be signs that the bowel is trapped, which needs emergency surgery.
:::

## How open repair works

In an open repair, I make one cut of a few centimetres in the groin, over the hernia. The bulge is pushed back into place and a flat mesh is fixed over the weak area from the front, to strengthen it.

Open repair can be done under a general anaesthetic, or under a local anaesthetic with sedation if needed. That makes it a good option for people who are less fit for a general anaesthetic. It also suits some large hernias that extend into the scrotum, and people who have had a previous keyhole repair on the same side.

## How keyhole hernia repair works

A keyhole (laparoscopic) repair is done under general anaesthetic. I make two or three small cuts, usually near the belly button and lower abdomen. Gas is used to create space, and a camera lets me see the hernia from behind. The mesh is placed on the inside of the muscle wall, covering the weak spot.

There are two main ways of doing this. In a **TEP** repair (totally extraperitoneal), the surgeon works in the layer between the muscles and the lining of the abdomen, without entering the abdomen itself. In a **TAPP** repair (transabdominal preperitoneal), the surgeon goes into the abdomen and opens the lining to place the mesh. Both are recognised by NICE. NICE also advises that keyhole repair should be done by surgeons who are trained in it and perform it regularly, and that patients should be told the risks and benefits of both approaches. It is a fair question to ask any surgeon how often they do it.

Keyhole repair is often my suggestion when:

- **There are hernias on both sides.** Both can be repaired through the same small cuts at one operation.
- **A hernia has come back after an open repair.** Approaching from behind means working through tissue that has not been operated on before.
- **Getting back to work or activity quickly matters.** Many people have less pain in the first days and return to normal activity a little sooner.

Previous surgery in the lower abdomen, some medical conditions or a very large hernia can make keyhole repair less suitable. Occasionally a keyhole operation has to be changed to an open one for safety. I always discuss that possibility before you consent.

## Is keyhole hernia repair safe?

Both operations are common and generally safe, but every operation has risks. The ones I discuss with patients include:

- bruising and swelling in the groin, and sometimes the scrotum, which can take some weeks to settle;
- a collection of fluid where the hernia was, which can feel like the hernia is still there for a while;
- wound infection;
- groin pain or numbness that lasts beyond three months, which affects a minority of people;
- the hernia coming back;
- rarely, damage to nearby structures such as the bowel, bladder or blood vessels, and blood clots.

Some of these risks differ slightly between the two approaches. Long-lasting groin pain is generally a little less common after keyhole repair, while the rare internal injuries are particular to the keyhole route because instruments enter the abdomen or the space behind it. I go through the risks that apply to you in clinic, so that the choice is yours.

## Recovery after keyhole and open repair

Most people go home the same day. Recovery is a little quicker after keyhole repair, but the overall pattern is similar.

- **The first few days.** Expect soreness and tiredness. Walking around the house from the first day helps.
- **Desk work.** Most people return within one to two weeks after keyhole repair. After open repair it can take a little longer.
- **Lifting and exercise.** Avoid heavy lifting and strenuous exercise for around four to six weeks. If your job involves heavy manual work, plan for more time off.
- **Driving.** You can drive once you can make an emergency stop comfortably. It is worth checking with your insurer too.
- **Full recovery.** The NHS guide to [inguinal hernia repair](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/inguinal-hernia-repair/) says full recovery usually takes up to four to six weeks.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is keyhole hernia repair better than open surgery?
Neither is better for everyone. Keyhole repair often means a quicker early recovery and suits hernias on both sides or recurrent hernias, while open repair can be done under local anaesthetic and suits some larger hernias.

### Do all inguinal hernias need surgery?
No. A hernia that causes no symptoms may not need treatment straight away, and watching it is a reasonable choice for some people. Surgery is usually advised if it causes pain, grows or affects daily life.

### How long after keyhole hernia repair can I go back to work?
Most people return to desk work within one to two weeks. Heavy lifting and strenuous exercise should wait for around four to six weeks.

### Will mesh be used?
Usually, yes. Mesh strengthens the weak area and lowers the chance of the hernia coming back. I discuss the type of repair and the mesh with you before surgery.
