Finding a lump in your breast is frightening. It is worth saying straight away: most breast lumps, roughly nine in ten, are benign. Cysts, fibroadenomas and ordinary nodular breast tissue are all far more common than cancer. But no examining finger, including mine, can reliably tell them apart. Diagnosis needs a process, and the process is quick.

## Don't grade it yourself

Patients often delay because a lump is painless, or mobile, or soft, features they have read are "reassuring". These rules of thumb are unreliable in both directions. The only sensible rule: any new lump, in a breast or armpit, gets assessed. The same goes for thickening, a change in the size of one breast, skin dimpling or a new nipple change.

## Triple assessment: the gold standard

Specialist breast clinics work to a standard called triple assessment:

- Clinical examination by a breast specialist
- Imaging: ultrasound for younger women, mammography with ultrasound for older women
- Needle biopsy of anything that needs a tissue answer

When all three point the same way, the diagnosis is highly reliable. This is exactly what a [one-stop clinic](~/procedures/benign-breast-conditions/) delivers in a single visit: you are examined, scanned and, if needed, biopsied on the day. Many patients leave already knowing the lump is a simple cyst (often drained there and then) or normal tissue.

## If it is cancer

For the minority whose biopsy shows cancer, early diagnosis is the reward for acting promptly. Small early cancers are usually treated with [breast-conserving surgery](~/procedures/breast-cancer-surgery/) and carry excellent long-term outcomes. The fortnight it takes to be seen and diagnosed will not change your outcome; the six months a lump sits in a drawer marked "probably nothing" might.

## The bottom line

See your GP, or [book directly](~/booking/) into a breast clinic if you are self-funding. Whichever route you choose: soon, not someday.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are most breast lumps cancer?
No. Around nine in ten breast lumps are benign, such as cysts or fibroadenomas. Every new lump should still be assessed, because they cannot be told apart by touch.

### How quickly should a breast lump be checked?
Promptly, within a couple of weeks. In the NHS, GPs refer under an urgent pathway; privately, a one-stop breast clinic can often see you within days.
