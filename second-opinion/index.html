Second opinions

A second opinion on breast cancer or breast surgery

An independent view from an experienced oncoplastic breast surgeon can confirm a plan, or show you options you had not been offered.

Request a second opinion
When to ask

Patients most often ask for a second opinion when:

  • A mastectomy has been advised and they want to know whether breast conserving or oncoplastic surgery is possible
  • They want to understand reconstruction before cancer surgery rather than after it
  • A benign diagnosis has been given but symptoms persist
  • They are considering risk reducing surgery because of a BRCA or other gene variant, or a strong family history
  • They are unhappy with the result of previous breast surgery

What Mr Ullah reviews

Your history and examination, your imaging and pathology, and where useful a request for further tests or a specialist review of the scans or slides. You receive a clear explanation of what he would recommend and why, with a letter for you and for your existing team.

Questions

Questions about second opinions

Will a second opinion delay my treatment?

Rarely. A private second opinion can usually be arranged within days, and for most breast cancers a short pause to be sure of the plan does not affect the outcome. If timing is critical in your case, Mr Ullah will say so plainly.

Do I need to tell my current team?

It is helpful but not required. Most consultants welcome a second opinion, and your team can share imaging and pathology to make the review complete.

What should I bring?

Clinic letters, imaging reports (and the images themselves if possible), biopsy or pathology reports, and a list of your questions. The private secretary can help to request records.

Is a second opinion covered by insurance?

Many UK insurers cover a second opinion consultation. Check with your insurer and obtain a pre-authorisation number first.
