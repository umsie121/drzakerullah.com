**In short:** breast pain on one side is common and is very rarely a sign of breast cancer when it is the only symptom. The usual causes are hormonal changes, pain from the muscles and joints of the chest wall, a cyst, or infection. Get checked if you also feel a lump or notice skin or nipple changes, if the breast is red and hot, or if pain stays in one spot.

Pain is one of the commonest reasons women are referred to my breast clinics, and pain on one side worries people more than pain in both. That is understandable. One-sided pain feels as if something specific must be wrong in that breast. Often it is not. In this post I go through what I actually find when I examine women with this symptom, and the signs that mean you should be seen promptly.

## Why breast pain on one side happens

Doctors split breast pain (the medical word is mastalgia) into two broad groups. The difference matters because the causes and the advice are different.

**Cyclical pain** is linked to your periods. It usually builds in the week or two before a period and eases once it starts. It tends to feel dull and heavy, often worse in the upper outer part of the breast, sometimes spreading into the armpit. NICE describes it as usually affecting both breasts, but in clinic I regularly see it worse on one side, or felt on one side only. It is caused by normal hormonal changes in breast tissue, not by disease.

**Non-cyclical pain** has no pattern with your cycle. It is more often felt on one side, and it is the group where a cause can sometimes be found. It is also the type seen after the menopause.

## Common causes of pain in one breast

These are the causes I see most often, roughly in order of how often I find them.

- **Chest wall pain.** A large share of "breast" pain actually comes from the muscles, ribs and cartilage underneath the breast, not the breast tissue itself. One form is costochondritis, an inflammation where the ribs join the breastbone. It is typically sharp or aching, sits on the inner side of the breast or towards the armpit, and is worse when you press on it, twist, lift or take a deep breath. A new exercise routine, heavy lifting, carrying a child on one hip or a long spell of coughing can all set it off.
- **Cyclical hormonal pain** felt more on one side, as above.
- **A cyst.** Breast cysts are fluid-filled sacs, most common from the late thirties to the menopause. They can appear quickly and be tender. They are benign and, if troublesome, can be drained with a fine needle.
- **Infection.** Mastitis and breast abscesses are most common during breastfeeding, but they also occur in women who are not breastfeeding, particularly smokers. The breast becomes red, hot, swollen and painful, and you may feel feverish.
- **Bra fit.** A bra that is too small, has a digging underwire, or gives too little support during exercise is a surprisingly frequent cause, and often worse on the larger breast.
- **Medicines.** Some contraceptive pills, HRT and certain antidepressants can cause breast tenderness.
- **An old injury or previous surgery.** A knock to the breast, or scar tissue after a biopsy or operation, can ache for months.
- **Pain from elsewhere.** Problems in the neck, upper back or shoulder can be felt in the breast. Shingles can cause pain on one side of the chest for a few days before the rash appears.

:::note
Chest pain that feels tight, heavy or crushing, spreads to your arm, jaw or back, or comes with breathlessness, sweating or feeling faint, is not breast pain. Call 999.
:::

## Is breast pain on one side a sign of cancer?

When pain is the only symptom, cancer is very unlikely. NICE guidance for GPs says that, without other worrying features such as a lump, skin changes or nipple discharge, the chance of breast cancer being the cause is very low. Pain on its own is not one of the reasons for an urgent suspected cancer referral in NICE's referral guidance, and current UK breast pain guidance is that pain alone, with a normal examination, does not usually need a scan.

I say this to patients plainly because it is true, and because anxiety about cancer often makes the pain feel worse. What I also tell them is this: pain does not rule cancer out either. If a cancer is present, it is usually something else, a lump or a change in the skin, that gives it away. That is why the examination matters more than the pain itself.

## When to get breast pain on one side checked

See your GP, or book a [breast clinic appointment](~/procedures/benign-breast-conditions/), if any of these apply:

- you feel a new lump or thickening in the breast or armpit;
- the skin is dimpled, puckered, or looks different;
- the nipple has turned in, has a rash, or is leaking fluid, especially if bloodstained;
- the pain stays in one particular spot and has not settled after a few weeks;
- the pain is new and different for you, and you have been through the menopause;
- painkillers and a better bra have not helped, or the pain is affecting your sleep or work.

Get advice the same day from your GP or NHS 111 if the breast is red, hot and swollen, or you have a fever or feel shivery. This can be an infection that needs antibiotics, and occasionally drainage.

## What happens when you are seen

I start with the story: where the pain is, when it started, whether it follows your cycle, what makes it worse, and your medicines and family history. Then I examine both breasts, the armpits and the chest wall. Pressing on the ribs and cartilage often reproduces the pain exactly, which tells us it is chest wall pain rather than a breast problem.

If the examination is normal and the pain is the only symptom, imaging is often not needed, and I will explain why. If there is a lump, a focal area of concern, or I am not completely satisfied, I arrange an ultrasound, a mammogram or both, depending on your age. At my [Rapid Access Breast Clinic at The Holly in Buckhurst Hill](~/news/rapid-access-breast-clinic-the-holly-buckhurst-hill/), this happens at the same visit, so you do not go home with an unanswered question.

For many women, a clear explanation is the treatment. Once they know it is a strained muscle or a hormonal pattern, the worry goes, and the pain becomes much easier to live with.

## What helps breast pain

- **A properly fitted bra.** A supportive bra during the day, a sports bra for exercise and a soft bra at night, if that is more comfortable. Get measured; sizes change with weight, pregnancy and age.
- **Simple painkillers.** Paracetamol or ibuprofen, or an anti-inflammatory gel rubbed onto the sore area, are the first things NICE suggests. Gel works particularly well for chest wall pain.
- **A pain diary.** Note the pain each day for two or three months, with your period dates. It shows whether there is a cyclical pattern and helps your doctor.
- **Rest the chest wall** if a particular activity triggers it, then return gradually.
- **Review your medicines** with your GP if the pain started with a new pill or HRT. Do not stop them without advice.

Evening primrose oil, vitamin E and cutting out caffeine are often suggested, but the evidence for them is limited, and NICE does not routinely recommend them. If the pain is still severe after about three months of these measures, a specialist can discuss other options. The [NHS page on breast pain](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/breast-pain/) is a good plain summary of self-help, and NICE's [summary on cyclical breast pain](https://cks.nice.org.uk/topics/breast-pain-cyclical/) sets out what GPs are advised to do.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why does only one of my breasts hurt?
Hormonal pain can be stronger on one side, and pain from the chest wall muscles and cartilage is usually one-sided. Cysts, infection and a poorly fitting bra are other common causes. Cancer is a rare cause when pain is the only symptom.

### Is pain in the left breast a sign of a heart problem?
Usually not. Breast and chest wall pain is often sharp, worse with movement and tender to touch. Call 999 if chest pain is tight or crushing, spreads to your arm or jaw, or comes with breathlessness or sweating.

### How long should breast pain last before I see a doctor?
See a doctor straight away if you have a lump, skin or nipple change, or a red, hot breast. If pain alone has not settled after a few weeks, stays in one spot, or is new after the menopause, get it checked.

### Do I need a mammogram for breast pain?
Not usually, if the pain is the only symptom and the examination is normal. Imaging is arranged if there is a lump, a focal area of concern or any doubt after examination.
