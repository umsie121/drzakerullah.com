General & laparoscopic surgery

Alongside his breast practice, Mr Ullah maintains a general surgical practice in laparoscopic — keyhole — surgery: gallbladder removal, hernia repair and appendicectomy.

Gallstones & gallbladder removal

Gallstones that cause pain, inflammation or pancreatitis are best treated by removing the gallbladder — laparoscopic cholecystectomy. Performed through four small incisions, it is one of the most common operations in the UK; most patients go home the same day or after one night and are back to normal activity within a fortnight. You can live entirely normally without a gallbladder.

Hernia repair

A hernia — in the groin (inguinal), at the navel (umbilical) or through a previous scar (incisional) — will not repair itself. Mr Ullah repairs hernias laparoscopically and by open surgery, usually reinforced with mesh, choosing the approach that suits the hernia and the patient. He has published research on laparoscopic hernia repair techniques.

Appendicectomy

Suspected appendicitis needs urgent assessment. Where surgery is required, laparoscopic appendicectomy allows both confirmation of the diagnosis and treatment through keyhole incisions, with a faster recovery than open surgery.

This page is general information, not medical advice. Whether a procedure is right for you can only be decided in consultation, after proper assessment.

FAQ

Common questions

What are the advantages of keyhole surgery?

Smaller incisions mean less pain after surgery, shorter hospital stays, faster return to work and smaller scars. Not every patient or condition is suitable, and a small proportion of keyhole operations need conversion to open surgery for safety — that possibility is always part of consent.

How long does hernia repair recovery take?

Most patients return to desk work within one to two weeks after laparoscopic repair, and to heavy lifting at around four to six weeks. Open repair recovery is somewhat longer. You can drive once you can perform an emergency stop comfortably.

Do gallstones always need surgery?

No. Stones found incidentally that cause no symptoms can usually be left alone. Surgery is advised once stones cause pain or complications, because further attacks are then likely.
