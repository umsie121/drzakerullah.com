Treatment 05

Breast augmentation

Augmentation uses an implant to increase the size or restore the fullness of the breast. Mr Ullah brings the judgement of an oncoplastic surgeon, who uses the same operation to restore symmetry after cancer.

An honest consultation

A good augmentation begins with a frank conversation about proportion: what an implant can and cannot do for your frame, how its size, profile and position over or under the muscle change the result, and how breast and implant will change over years rather than months. You will receive the same measured advice Mr Ullah gives every patient.

Augmentation after cancer

Augmentation is also a reconstructive tool. After breast conserving surgery or reconstruction, an implant on one or both sides can restore volume and symmetry. Patients with a personal or family history of breast cancer are given specific advice on imaging and follow up with implants in place.

The operation and aftercare

The operation takes around an hour under general anaesthetic and is usually a day case. Most patients return to desk work within a week and to full exercise at four to six weeks. Implants are not lifetime devices and most women will need further surgery at some point. Mr Ullah explains the long term picture, including rare implant related conditions, before you decide.

This page is general information and not medical advice. Whether a procedure is right for you can only be decided in consultation.

Questions

Questions about breast augmentation

How long do breast implants last?

Implants do not need replacing on a fixed schedule, but they do not last forever. Many women have further surgery within 10 to 15 years for wear, capsular contracture or a change of preference. It is best to plan for maintenance over a lifetime.

Are breast implants safe?

Modern implants are well studied and serious complications are rare, but not unknown. They include capsular contracture, rupture and BIA-ALCL, a rare lymphoma associated with some textured implants. Mr Ullah explains the current evidence so that your decision is fully informed.

Can I still have mammograms with implants?

Yes. Screening remains effective, although implants can obscure some breast tissue and radiographers take additional views. Always tell the screening service that you have implants.
