**In short:** an online breast surgeon consultation works well for results, a second opinion, a family history question or follow up after treatment. It cannot replace an examination. If you have a new lump or another new breast change, you need to be seen in person, examined and usually scanned, so video is a first step, not the whole assessment.

Since the pandemic, many people expect to see a doctor on a screen, and for some appointments that is a real improvement. No travel, no time off work, and a relative in another city can join the call. I offer video and telephone consultations, in English or Bangla, and I use them often.

I also turn some requests for a video appointment into a face to face one. That is not about inconvenience. Breast medicine depends heavily on what I can feel and what a scan shows, and a camera gives me neither. This post explains where the line sits, so you can choose the right kind of appointment the first time.

## When an online breast surgeon consultation works well

Video is at its best when the information I need is already in words, reports or images, rather than in your breast tissue. In my practice that usually means:

- **Discussing results.** If your scans or biopsy have been done, we can go through what they show and what happens next. Many people find it easier to take in difficult news at home, with family beside them.
- **A second opinion.** If you have a diagnosis and a treatment plan from another team, I can review your clinic letters, imaging and pathology reports and talk them through with you. I have written more about this in [getting a second opinion on breast cancer](~/news/breast-cancer-second-opinion/).
- **Family history and risk.** A conversation about breast cancer in your family, what it might mean for you and whether genetic testing or extra screening is worth considering is mostly a careful history. That suits video well.
- **Planning surgery.** Once a diagnosis is clear, we can talk through the options, the risks and the recovery before you come in.
- **Follow up.** After an operation or after treatment has finished, some follow up appointments are simply a check on how you are doing, and they do not always need a trip to clinic.
- **Patients living abroad or far away.** For someone overseas, or with family abroad who want to be part of the discussion, video makes a specialist conversation possible at all. Any treatment then takes place at one of my UK clinics.

## When you need to be seen in person

Any new breast symptom in the UK is assessed by what is called triple assessment: examination by a breast specialist, imaging (an ultrasound scan, a mammogram, which is a low dose breast X-ray, or both) and, where needed, a needle biopsy, where a small sample of tissue is taken under local anaesthetic. Two of those three steps cannot happen over a video link.

So I would ask you to book a face to face appointment if you have:

- a new lump in the breast or armpit;
- a change in the shape, size or skin of the breast, such as dimpling, puckering or redness;
- a nipple that has newly turned in, or a rash on the nipple;
- nipple discharge, especially if it is bloodstained or comes from one side only;
- breast pain in one spot that does not settle.

Most of these changes turn out to be benign. The point of an examination and a scan is to give you a clear answer rather than a guess. You can read more about how I approach [breast lumps, breast pain and other benign breast conditions](~/procedures/benign-breast-conditions/), and about [when a breast lump needs a specialist](~/news/breast-lump-when-to-see-a-specialist/).

If you have one of these symptoms and are not sure what to book, a short call can still be useful to plan the visit. But please do not let a video appointment delay an examination. If you are worried about breast cancer, your GP can also refer you urgently on the NHS.

## What video cannot tell me

It can be tempting to think that a careful description, or holding a lump up to the camera, is almost as good. It is not, for three reasons.

First, the feel of a lump matters. Whether it is smooth or irregular, mobile or fixed, soft or hard, gives me information that no description captures. Second, many important findings are subtle, such as a slight skin tethering or a small node in the armpit, and are easy to miss even in person without a proper examination. Third, the answer for nearly every new symptom comes from imaging, and that needs you in a clinic.

This is why I am cautious. A reassuring video call that is not followed by an examination can give false comfort, and that is the outcome I most want to avoid.

## Consultations in English or Bangla

I consult in English and Bangla. For many families, discussing a diagnosis in their first language changes the whole conversation. Questions get asked that would otherwise go unasked, and older relatives can follow the plan without someone translating every sentence.

Video helps here too. Relatives in another city or another country can join the same call. If it would help, tell my secretary which language you would prefer when you book.

## How to prepare for a video consultation

A few simple steps make the appointment much more useful.

1. **Send your records first.** Clinic letters, imaging and biopsy reports and, if you have them, the images themselves. Reports should be in English or come with a translation.
2. **Write down your questions.** It is easy to forget them once the call begins.
3. **List your medicines,** including any hormone treatment or contraception.
4. **Find a quiet, private room** with a steady internet connection, and charge your device.
5. **Decide who you would like with you.** A partner, friend or relative can join, in the room or on the call.

All the practical details, including how records are sent and how the written opinion afterwards works, are on my [video consultations page](~/remote-consultations/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a breast surgeon diagnose a lump over video?
No. A new lump needs an examination and usually an ultrasound or mammogram, and sometimes a biopsy. A video call can help plan that visit, but it cannot replace it.

### What is an online breast surgeon consultation useful for?
Discussing results, a second opinion on a diagnosis or treatment plan, family history and risk, planning surgery, and some follow up appointments.

### Can my family join a video consultation?
Yes. Relatives can join from home or from abroad, and I consult in English or Bangla.

### Is a video consultation covered by insurance?
Some UK insurers cover remote consultations, but not all policies do. Check with your insurer before booking.
