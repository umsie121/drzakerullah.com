**In short:** I see private patients at Nuffield Health at St Bartholomew's Hospital, 38 Giltspur Street, EC1A 7BS, a short walk from Farringdon, Barbican and St Paul's. Consultations, breast imaging and day case surgery are on the same site, so a breast clinic in the City of London can fit around a working day. Insured and self-funding patients are welcome.

Many of the people I see at St Bartholomew's work within a mile or so of Smithfield. They come from offices in the Square Mile, studios in Clerkenwell and Shoreditch, chambers and firms around Holborn, and from the streets around Liverpool Street and Farringdon. Others live in Islington, Hackney or Tower Hamlets and find the City easier to reach than anywhere else.

A breast change rarely arrives at a convenient moment. This post explains what it is like to be seen in the City: where the clinic is, how the site works, what the first appointment involves, and what happens if you need tests or an operation.

## Where the clinic is, and how to get there

Nuffield Health's private hospital sits within the St Bartholomew's Hospital site in Smithfield. The address is 38 Giltspur Street, London EC1A 7BS. Barts, as most Londoners call it, has cared for patients on this spot for centuries, and today it is home to one of the country's major cancer centres. The private hospital is run by Nuffield Health and is separate from the NHS hospital around it.

Getting there from different parts of the City is usually straightforward:

- **Farringdon.** A few minutes on foot. Farringdon is served by the Elizabeth line, Thameslink and the Circle, Hammersmith and City and Metropolitan lines, so it is often the easiest route from Clerkenwell, King's Cross or further afield.
- **Barbican and St Paul's.** Both are a short walk away, on the Circle, Hammersmith and City and Metropolitan lines at Barbican, and the Central line at St Paul's.
- **Liverpool Street and Moorgate.** A few minutes away by Tube or on foot. For people working around Broadgate, Bishopsgate or Spitalfields this is often the simplest route.
- **Holborn and Chancery Lane.** Within walking distance along Holborn Viaduct for many people in legal and professional offices.
- **Shoreditch.** A short bus ride or a walk through the City.

## Why a breast clinic in the City of London can save you time

The thing my City patients value most is that so much happens in one place. At St Bartholomew's I can see you, arrange imaging, and if it is needed, carry out day case surgery on the same site. For many people that means fewer separate trips and less time away from work.

When you come with a new breast symptom, the standard approach in the UK is triple assessment:

1. **Examination.** I ask about the change you have noticed, how long it has been there, your health, your medicines and your family history. Then I examine both breasts and both armpits. A chaperone is always offered.
2. **Imaging.** Depending on your age and what I find, this is an ultrasound scan, a mammogram (a low dose breast X-ray), or both.
3. **A needle biopsy, if needed.** A small sample of tissue is taken under local anaesthetic and sent to the laboratory.

When all three agree, the answer is very reliable. Most breast symptoms turn out to be benign, such as cysts, fibroadenomas or normal changes in breast tissue. I have written more about the conditions I see most often on my page on [breast lumps, pain and benign breast conditions](~/procedures/benign-breast-conditions/), and about [when a breast lump needs a specialist](~/news/breast-lump-when-to-see-a-specialist/).

There is more detail on what to expect from an appointment, including what to bring, on my [your visit page](~/your-visit/).

## Insurance and self-funding

Many City employers offer private medical insurance, and a good number of my patients are using it for the first time.

- **If you are insured,** most insurers ask for a GP referral letter and a pre-authorisation number before your first appointment. Call your insurer's helpline, explain that you have a new breast symptom, and ask what your policy covers.
- **If you are with Bupa,** I am a Bupa Platinum consultant and fee-assured, so you should not face a shortfall on my fees for covered treatment. I am also part of Bupa's Open Referral network, and you can ask for me by name.
- **Other insurers** I am recognised by include Bupa Global, AXA Health, Aviva, Vitality, WPA, Cigna, Healix and Health-on-Line.
- **If you are paying for yourself,** you do not need a referral or insurance. The consultation fee is confirmed when you book, and any tests or surgery are priced in writing before anything goes ahead.

The details are on my [fees and insurance page](~/fees-and-insurance/). With your permission, I write to your GP after every visit so your NHS record stays complete.

## If you need treatment

If tests show something that needs treatment, you stay with me from diagnosis through to follow-up. That might be removing a benign lump that is causing discomfort or worry, or a minor procedure under local anaesthetic.

If a biopsy shows cancer, your case is discussed by a multidisciplinary team of surgeons, radiologists, pathologists, oncologists and specialist nurses, who agree a plan together. As an oncoplastic breast surgeon, I plan the cancer operation and the appearance of the breast at the same time. You can read about the options on my [breast cancer surgery page](~/procedures/breast-cancer-surgery/).

Some operations suit day case surgery at St Bartholomew's. Others, particularly larger operations or reconstruction, may be better carried out at Nuffield Health The Holly in Buckhurst Hill, which has full operating theatres and parking on site. We decide together, based on the operation and on where you would rather recover. Some people want everything near the office. Others would rather be close to home and family afterwards.

My NHS work is as Clinical Lead of the Breast Unit at Whipps Cross University Hospital, part of Barts Health NHS Trust. That does not change your private care, but it does mean I know the NHS pathways well and can explain them honestly if you are weighing up the two.

## Seeing me for a second opinion in the City

Because the City is easy to reach from most of London, St Bartholomew's is where many people come when they would like a [second opinion](~/second-opinion/) on a diagnosis or a proposed operation. A second opinion is a normal, reasonable request. It is most useful when I can see your scans, biopsy reports and clinic letters, so please bring copies or ask your team to send them before the appointment.

If you cannot easily get away from work for a first conversation, some appointments, such as going through results or follow-up, can be done by video. An examination still needs a face to face visit. More on this is on my [video consultations page](~/remote-consultations/).

:::note
If you have noticed a new lump, a change in the shape of a breast, skin dimpling, or a change in a nipple, please book promptly or see your GP rather than waiting for a quieter week at work. The NHS has a clear guide to [breast cancer symptoms](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/breast-cancer-in-women/symptoms/).
:::

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a private breast clinic in the City of London?
Yes. I see patients at Nuffield Health at St Bartholomew's Hospital, 38 Giltspur Street, London EC1A 7BS. It is a few minutes on foot from Farringdon, Barbican and St Paul's, and a few minutes from Liverpool Street and Moorgate.

### Can I have a mammogram and ultrasound at St Bartholomew's on the same visit?
Imaging is available on the St Bartholomew's site, so where it is needed it can usually be arranged quickly and close by. If anything needs sampling, a biopsy can follow.

### Is the private hospital part of the NHS St Bartholomew's Hospital?
No. Nuffield Health runs the private hospital on the St Bartholomew's site. It is separate from the NHS hospital, though both are on the same historic campus in Smithfield.

### Do I need a GP referral to be seen in the City?
If you are using insurance, most insurers ask for one along with a pre-authorisation number. If you are paying for yourself, you can book directly without a referral.
