Treatment 08

Lumps, cysts, moles and skin lesions

Lumps under the skin and changing moles are common reasons to see a surgeon. Mr Ullah assesses and removes them, usually as a short procedure under local anaesthetic, with every specimen sent for analysis.

What Mr Ullah treats

  • Lipomas: soft, slow growing lumps of fat under the skin
  • Sebaceous (epidermoid) cysts, including those that keep becoming inflamed
  • Moles and skin lesions that are changing, bleeding, catching on clothing or simply unwanted
  • Abscesses, which need prompt drainage
  • Other soft tissue lumps that need a diagnosis

Assessment first

Most lumps under the skin are harmless, but a few need a different plan. Mr Ullah examines the lump and, where it helps, arranges an ultrasound scan before deciding on treatment. A lump that is large, deep, growing quickly or painful is always imaged first. Any mole with features of concern is removed completely and examined in the laboratory, and if melanoma is confirmed, your care continues with the appropriate specialist team.

The procedure

Most lumps, cysts and moles are removed under local anaesthetic, through an incision planned to leave the neatest possible scar, closed with fine stitches. You go home the same day. Stitches usually come out after one to two weeks, and the laboratory report follows within a similar time.

This page is general information and not medical advice. Whether a procedure is right for you can only be decided in consultation.

Questions

Questions about lumps, cysts and moles

Is a lipoma dangerous?

Lipomas are benign, and many can be left alone. They are usually removed when they are growing, uncomfortable or unwanted. A lump that is large, deep, painful or growing quickly should be scanned first to be sure of the diagnosis.

Will removing a sebaceous cyst leave a scar?

There is always a small scar, usually a fine line a little longer than the cyst. Removing the whole cyst wall reduces the chance of it coming back.

Should a changing mole be checked?

Yes. A mole that is changing in size, shape or colour, has an irregular edge, bleeds or itches should be seen promptly. Most are harmless, but a mole of concern is removed and examined in the laboratory.

Is minor skin surgery covered by insurance?

Insurers usually cover the removal of lesions that are suspicious or causing symptoms, but not purely cosmetic removal. Self-funding patients receive a fixed price from the hospital in advance.
