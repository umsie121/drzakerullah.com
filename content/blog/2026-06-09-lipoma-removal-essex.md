Lipomas are one of the most common things people bring to my general surgery clinic. Usually they have lived with the lump for years. It has slowly got a little bigger, it catches on a bra strap or a rucksack, or they have simply had enough of it. Sometimes a friend has said "you should get that looked at", and they want to know if it is anything to worry about. Most of the time, it is not.

## What is a lipoma?

A lipoma is a collection of normal fat cells wrapped in a thin capsule, sitting just under the skin. It is benign, which means it is not a cancer and does not spread. Typically a lipoma:

- feels soft or rubbery, and moves a little under your fingers
- grows very slowly, over months or years
- is painless, although it can ache if it presses on something
- appears on the shoulders, back, neck, upper arms, chest or thighs, though it can turn up almost anywhere

Some people have one. Others have several, and a few families seem to be prone to them. Lipomas are not caused by diet or weight, and losing weight does not usually shrink them.

## When should a lipoma be checked?

Any new lump under the skin is worth showing to a doctor once, even if you are fairly sure what it is. The [NHS advice on lipomas](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/lipoma/) suggests seeing your GP if a lump is painful, red or hot, hard and fixed, or getting bigger.

The reason for care is a rare group of cancers called **soft tissue sarcomas**. In the early stages they can feel like a lipoma. They are uncommon, and most lumps are nothing of the sort, but they are the reason surgeons are cautious. NICE guidance (NG12) asks GPs to consider an urgent ultrasound scan, within two weeks, for an adult with an unexplained lump that is increasing in size. Specialists also look harder at a lump that:

- is larger than about 5 cm across, roughly the size of a golf ball
- lies deep, under the muscle layer rather than just under the skin
- feels hard, or does not move
- is painful
- has grown noticeably over weeks or a few months

In my clinic, any lump with one of these features has an ultrasound before I plan an operation. The scan usually confirms a simple lipoma, and then we carry on. If it does not, the lump may need an MRI scan or a biopsy, and if a sarcoma is suspected you are referred to a specialist sarcoma team. I would much rather scan ten harmless lipomas than cut into one sarcoma without a plan, because how a sarcoma is first operated on affects the result.

## Will the NHS remove a lipoma?

Sometimes. The NHS generally removes a lipoma when it is causing real problems: it is growing, painful, pressing on something, or the diagnosis is uncertain. Many local NHS areas in Essex and London treat the removal of a small, harmless lipoma as a cosmetic procedure that is not routinely funded, and your GP may need to apply for approval. Rules vary between areas, so your GP is the best person to ask.

A lipoma that might not be a lipoma is a different matter. If there is any doubt about the diagnosis, the NHS will assess it properly, and quickly when the features above are present.

Privately, you can have a lipoma removed because it bothers you, whatever its size. Insurers usually cover removal when a lump is symptomatic or the diagnosis is uncertain, but not when the reason is purely cosmetic. Check with your insurer first. If you are self-funding, the hospital gives you a fixed price in advance. My [lumps, cysts and skin lesions page](~/procedures/lumps-cysts-skin-lesions/) has more on how this works.

## Lipoma removal in Essex: what happens on the day

Most of my patients from Loughton, Chigwell, Woodford Green and Epping have their lipoma removed at [Nuffield Health The Holly Hospital in Buckhurst Hill](~/locations/buckhurst-hill-essex/), which has parking on site and is a short walk from Buckhurst Hill Central line station. I also operate in central London for patients who prefer it.

1. **Consultation.** I examine the lump, ask how long it has been there and whether it has changed, and arrange a scan if it needs one. We talk about where the scar will sit.
2. **Local anaesthetic.** For most lipomas you stay awake. The area is numbed with an injection, which stings for a few seconds. After that you should feel pushing but not pain.
3. **Removal.** I make a cut along the natural skin lines, free the lipoma from the surrounding tissue and lift it out whole. Because fat is soft, a lipoma can sometimes be eased out through a cut a little shorter than the lump itself.
4. **Closing.** The skin is closed with fine stitches and covered with a dressing.

The procedure itself often takes 20 to 45 minutes, and you go home the same day. A very large or deep lipoma, or one in an awkward place, may be better removed under general anaesthetic, and I will tell you in advance if I think that applies to you. Every lipoma I remove is sent to the laboratory, so we know for certain what it was.

## Recovery and the scar

Most people go back to desk work within a day or two. Expect some soreness and bruising for a few days, usually helped by simple painkillers such as paracetamol. If the lipoma was on your back or shoulder, avoid heavy lifting and stretching that area for a week or two, so the wound is not pulled apart.

Stitches usually come out after one to two weeks, at your GP surgery or back at the hospital. The laboratory report comes back within a similar time, and I let you know the result. Like any operation, there are small risks: bleeding under the skin, a collection of fluid, infection or a wider scar than hoped. Lipomas that are removed whole do not often come back.

There is always a scar. It starts pink and firm and usually fades over the following year into a fine pale line. Keeping it out of strong sun for that first year helps. A scar on the back or shoulders tends to stretch a little more than one elsewhere, and I will tell you honestly what to expect for your particular lump.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a lipoma turn into cancer?
A lipoma does not turn into cancer. The concern is that a rare cancer called a sarcoma can occasionally look like a lipoma at first, which is why large, deep, hard or growing lumps are scanned before removal.

### Is lipoma removal painful?
The local anaesthetic injection stings briefly, then the area goes numb. Afterwards most people need only simple painkillers for a few days.

### How long does it take to recover from lipoma removal?
Most people return to light work within a day or two. Stitches come out after one to two weeks, and the scar continues to soften and fade over about a year.

### Will a lipoma go away on its own?
Lipomas rarely shrink or disappear without treatment. Many people choose to leave a small one alone, which is perfectly safe once the diagnosis is clear.
