Breast lumps, pain & benign breast conditions

Most breast symptoms turn out to be benign — but every new symptom deserves a proper answer, quickly. Mr Ullah introduced the one-stop clinic at Whipps Cross, and runs his private clinics the same way: examination, imaging and, if needed, biopsy at a single visit.

Symptoms Mr Ullah assesses

Patients are most commonly seen with:

  • A new lump or area of thickening in the breast or armpit
  • Breast pain (mastalgia) — cyclical, constant, or in one spot
  • Nipple discharge, nipple inversion or a rash on the nipple
  • Skin dimpling, puckering or redness
  • Breast cysts and fibroadenomas
  • A change in breast size or shape
  • A family history of breast cancer, and questions about personal risk

The one-stop approach

The traditional route to a diagnosis — clinic, then a separate scan, then another appointment for results — stretches an anxious wait across weeks. At a one-stop clinic Mr Ullah takes a full history and examines you, arranges imaging (ultrasound and/or mammography, depending on your age and symptoms) at the same visit, and performs a needle biopsy there and then if anything needs sampling.

This is triple assessment — the UK gold standard for breast symptoms. Most patients leave their first appointment either fully reassured or with a clear plan.

If treatment is needed

Many benign conditions need no treatment once a firm diagnosis is made. Where treatment helps, Mr Ullah offers cyst aspiration, excision of symptomatic fibroadenomas or other benign lumps, duct surgery for troublesome nipple discharge, and structured, evidence-based advice for breast pain.

If a biopsy does show cancer, you are already under the care of an experienced oncoplastic breast cancer surgeon — the plan moves forward without changing hands.

FAQ

Common questions

Is a breast lump usually cancer?

No. The large majority of breast lumps are benign — cysts, fibroadenomas or normal nodular tissue. But there is no reliable way to tell by touch alone, which is why every new lump should be assessed with examination and imaging, and biopsied if needed.

What is triple assessment?

Triple assessment is the standard of care for breast symptoms: clinical examination, imaging (mammogram and/or ultrasound), and a needle biopsy where indicated. When all three agree, the diagnosis is highly reliable. A one-stop clinic delivers all three in a single visit.

How quickly can I be seen privately?

Patients with new breast symptoms are prioritised. Appointments are booked directly with the hospital you choose, and can often be arranged within days.

Should I worry about breast pain?

Breast pain on its own is very rarely a sign of cancer, and is one of the most common reasons women see a breast specialist. Pain that is persistent and in one specific spot is still worth assessing, and a clear diagnosis is often the most effective treatment of all.

Do I need a GP referral?

Insured patients usually need a GP referral for their insurer to authorise the appointment. Self-funding patients can generally book directly with the hospital.
