**In short:** a rubbery, slightly tender lump directly behind the nipple in a man is most often **gynaecomastia**, a benign growth of breast gland tissue. But men do get breast cancer, and the only way to know for sure is a proper breast assessment. Any new lump should be checked, and you can [book an assessment](~/booking/) directly.

Men are often embarrassed to mention a breast lump, or assume it can't be serious. I see men of every age in clinic, from teenagers to retired men, and the conversation is always routine.

## What gynaecomastia usually feels like

- A disc of firm, rubbery tissue centred under the nipple
- Often on both sides, though it may start on one side
- Sometimes tender, especially in the first few months
- Moves with the nipple rather than feeling fixed or hard

Common causes include the hormonal changes of puberty and later life, weight gain, certain medications (some used for prostate, heart or stomach conditions), anabolic steroids and some medical conditions affecting the liver, kidneys or thyroid.

## Signs that need prompt assessment

Male breast cancer is uncommon. In the UK, a few hundred men are diagnosed each year, compared with tens of thousands of women. See a doctor promptly if you notice:

- A hard, painless lump, particularly one that is off-centre from the nipple
- A lump on one side only that is growing
- Nipple discharge, especially bloodstained, or a newly pulled-in nipple
- Skin changes, an ulcer or a rash on the nipple
- A lump in the armpit

## How it is assessed

The process is the same [triple assessment](~/procedures/benign-breast-conditions/) used for women. That means examination by a breast specialist, an ultrasound and, if needed, a mammogram, plus a needle biopsy for anything uncertain. For most men the answer is gynaecomastia, given at the same visit.

## If it is gynaecomastia

Teenage gynaecomastia often settles on its own within a year or two. If a medication is the cause, it may settle once a safe alternative is found. Established gynaecomastia that troubles you can be treated with [surgery](~/procedures/gynaecomastia/). Liposuction removes the fatty component, and the firm gland itself usually needs to be excised through a small incision at the edge of the areola. A breast surgeon who also treats male breast cancer is well placed to make sure nothing is missed.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a lump behind the nipple in a man always gynaecomastia?
Not always. It usually is, but male breast cancer does occur, so any new lump should be examined and scanned.

### Can gynaecomastia go away on its own?
Yes, often in teenagers and sometimes when a causative medication is stopped. Long-standing gynaecomastia rarely resolves without treatment.

### Who should I see about a male breast lump?
A breast surgeon. They can assess the lump fully, rule out cancer and, if needed, treat gynaecomastia.
