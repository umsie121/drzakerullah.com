What is oncoplastic breast surgery?

Oncoplastic surgery removes breast cancer and reshapes the breast in one operation. A consultant oncoplastic surgeon explains how it works and if it is safe.

16 July 2026

Reading time

3 minutes

Medically reviewed

3 November 2026

By Mr Md Zaker Ullah FRCS, Consultant Oncoplastic Breast Surgeon.

For most of the twentieth century, breast cancer surgery had one objective: remove the cancer. What the breast looked like afterwards was, at best, a secondary concern. Oncoplastic surgery, the field in which I have spent my consultant career, rejects that trade-off. It plans the cancer operation and the appearance of the breast together, in the same operation, by the same surgeon.

Two disciplines, one operation

The word itself is the method: onco (cancer) + plastic (reshaping). An oncoplastic surgeon removes the tumour with the clear margins that cancer control demands, and then immediately uses plastic surgical techniques to reconstruct, reshape or rebalance the breast, rather than simply closing the wound and hoping.

What it makes possible

  • Larger safe excisions. Reshaping the breast during surgery means more tissue can be removed without leaving a deformity, so some women who would once have needed mastectomy can keep their breast.
  • Therapeutic mammoplasty. For women with larger breasts, the cancer can be removed as part of a breast reduction, treating the cancer and leaving a lighter, lifted breast.
  • Immediate reconstruction. Where mastectomy is necessary, skin-sparing techniques and immediate reconstruction mean waking with the breast shape already restored.
  • Symmetry as part of the plan. Adjusting the other breast is considered from the start, not left as an afterthought.

Does the cosmetic focus compromise the cancer surgery?

No, and this is the point most worth making. The evidence is consistent that oncoplastic breast-conserving surgery achieves cancer control at least equivalent to standard techniques; the freedom to excise generously may even improve margins. The cancer operation always comes first. The oncoplastic discipline is about refusing to accept that a well-treated cancer requires a poorly treated breast.

Questions to ask your surgeon

If you are facing breast cancer surgery, ask: What will my breast look like afterwards? What are my options if the excision will be large? If I need a mastectomy, what reconstruction could happen at the same operation? A surgeon working in the oncoplastic tradition will have concrete answers before the operation, not apologies after it. If you do not get them, a second opinion is always reasonable.

Questions

Is oncoplastic surgery as safe as standard breast cancer surgery?

Yes. Studies show cancer control after oncoplastic breast-conserving surgery is at least as good as with standard techniques.

Is oncoplastic surgery covered by insurance?

When performed as part of breast cancer treatment, oncoplastic surgery and symmetrising surgery to the other breast are usually covered by UK private medical insurers. Always confirm with your insurer.
