Treatment 03

Breast reconstruction

Reconstruction restores the shape of the breast after cancer surgery, either at the same operation or years later. Mr Ullah offers the full range of implant and tissue techniques, and is equally clear when no reconstruction is the right choice.

Written and medically reviewed by Mr Md Zaker Ullah FRCS. Updated 4 November 2026.

Immediate or delayed

Immediate reconstruction takes place during the same operation as the mastectomy, so the shape of the breast is restored before you wake. Delayed reconstruction can be carried out months or years later; there is no time limit. The right choice depends on the cancer treatment planned, radiotherapy in particular, on your health and on your own preference. Some women choose not to have a reconstruction. That is a valid decision, and Mr Ullah will say so.

Implant reconstruction

The most common technique in the United Kingdom uses a silicone implant, sometimes supported by a mesh or tissue matrix, to recreate the breast. It avoids surgery elsewhere on the body and has a shorter recovery. In return, the breast feels less natural and is likely to need further surgery over a lifetime.

Reconstruction with your own tissue

A reconstruction made from your own tissue is warm and natural, and changes with you over time. Mr Ullah performs latissimus dorsi (LD) flap reconstruction, which brings muscle and skin from the back to the chest and is often combined with an implant. For suitable cancers near the nipple he also uses the Grisotti flap, which rebuilds the area from the breast's own tissue during the cancer operation.

Chest wall perforator flaps

After breast conserving surgery, a dent or loss of shape can be corrected with a chest wall perforator flap (LICAP or MICAP). Tissue from just beside the breast, on its own small blood supply, is moved into the defect, restoring natural shape without an implant and without surgery elsewhere on the body. It is an area of particular expertise for Mr Ullah.

Completing the result

Reconstruction is often completed in stages: fat grafting to refine the contour, nipple reconstruction with a small local flap, and symmetrising surgery to the other breast, usually a reduction or an uplift, so that the two sit naturally together.

This page is general information and not medical advice. Whether a procedure is right for you can only be decided in consultation.

Questions

Questions about breast reconstruction

Will a reconstructed breast look and feel natural?

Reconstruction restores shape and symmetry in clothes very reliably, and modern techniques can look natural without clothes too. Sensation is usually reduced. Reconstructions made from your own tissue generally feel more natural than implants.

Is reconstruction safe if I need radiotherapy?

Radiotherapy increases the rate of complications for some types of reconstruction, implants in particular. If radiotherapy is likely, it shapes the recommended technique or timing, which is why reconstruction is planned alongside cancer treatment.

Can I have reconstruction years after my mastectomy?

Yes. Delayed reconstruction is routine and there is no time limit. Women treated ten or more years ago still have options, depending on their health and previous treatment.

Is breast reconstruction covered by insurance?

Reconstruction after breast cancer, including surgery to the other breast for symmetry, is normally covered by UK private medical insurers as part of cancer treatment. Confirm with your insurer beforehand.
