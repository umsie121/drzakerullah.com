In this episode

The five questions

A short written summary of each topic, with links to more detail.

1. Lumpectomy or mastectomy — how is the decision made?

It depends on the size of the cancer relative to the breast, where it sits, whether there is more than one area, genetics and, crucially, the patient's own wishes. Oncoplastic techniques mean many women who once needed mastectomy can now keep their breast. Breast cancer surgery explained.

2. What symptoms should I look for, and how should I self-check?

Not just a lump: skin dimpling, a change in size or shape, a newly inverted nipple, nipple rash or bloodstained discharge, and persistent pain in one spot. Know what is normal for you and get any change checked. When to see a specialist.

3. Is breast cancer rising in young women?

UK data show breast cancer incidence has risen in younger women in recent decades, although it remains far less common under 40. Young women should never be told they are ‘too young’ to have a new symptom assessed.

4. Does everyone need radiotherapy?

No. Radiotherapy is standard after most breast-conserving surgery, and selectively after mastectomy, but some lower-risk patients can safely omit it. The decision is made by the multidisciplinary team using the tumour's biology and stage.

5. What is triple-negative breast cancer?

A type of breast cancer that lacks oestrogen, progesterone and HER2 receptors, so hormone and HER2-targeted drugs do not work. It is often treated with chemotherapy, sometimes with immunotherapy, before surgery, and responds well to modern regimens in many patients.
