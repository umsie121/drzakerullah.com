Private breast clinic near Woodford Green: The Holly explained

A private breast clinic near Woodford Green: getting to The Holly, what the appointment involves, insurance or self-funding, and how to book.

11 November 2025

Reading time

By Mr Md Zaker Ullah FRCS, Consultant Oncoplastic Breast Surgeon.

In short: the nearest private breast clinic to Woodford Green is at Nuffield Health The Holly Hospital, a short drive up the High Road in Buckhurst Hill. I see patients there on Tuesday afternoons and Thursday mornings. You can come insured, usually with a GP referral, or self-funding without one, and most new symptoms are assessed in a single visit.

A good number of the people I see at The Holly live within a couple of miles of it, in Woodford Green, Woodford Wells and South Woodford. Many already know Whipps Cross, where I lead the NHS breast unit, and some have been my NHS patients before. This post is for them: the practical side of coming to see me privately, from the drive up the High Road to what happens after you leave.

If you want the fuller picture of the Tuesday clinic itself, I have written a separate guide to the Rapid Access Breast Clinic at The Holly. Here I want to answer the questions local patients tend to ask on the phone before they book.

Getting to The Holly from Woodford Green and South Woodford

The Holly is on the High Road in Buckhurst Hill, IG9 5HX, on the edge of Epping Forest. If you live in Woodford Green or Woodford Wells you are, in effect, on the same road: carry on north along the High Road and the hospital is on your way towards Loughton.

  • By car. There is parking on site, which makes a real difference when you are already anxious about the appointment. If you are coming from further afield, the M11 and M25 are a few minutes away.
  • By Tube. From South Woodford, the Central line runs towards Epping through Woodford to Buckhurst Hill. The hospital is a short walk from Buckhurst Hill station.
  • If you would rather not drive afterwards. Most breast clinic appointments, including a needle biopsy, do not stop you driving home. Some patients still like to bring someone with them, for company as much as transport, and you are welcome to.

Full address details and a map are on the Buckhurst Hill clinic page.

What the appointment involves

The clinic works as a one-stop clinic. The idea is simple: rather than a consultation one week, a scan the next and results a fortnight later, as much as possible happens at the same visit.

  1. We talk first. I ask about the symptom, how long it has been there, whether it changes with your cycle, any previous breast problems and your family history. I also want to know what is worrying you most, because that shapes what I explain.
  2. I examine both breasts and both armpits. A chaperone is always offered.
  3. Imaging, if you need it. Depending on your age and what I find, that is an ultrasound, a mammogram or both, usually on the same day.
  4. A needle biopsy, if anything needs sampling. This is done under local anaesthetic, guided by ultrasound, and takes a few minutes.

Examination, imaging and biopsy together are called triple assessment. It is the standard for any new breast symptom in the UK, and it is how I assess breast lumps, pain and other benign breast conditions. Most patients leave either reassured or with a clear next step.

I consult in English and Bengali. For some families, particularly when an older relative is the patient, being able to talk it through in Bengali takes a lot of the strain out of the visit.

Questions about your own situation? A consultation with Mr Ullah can often be arranged within days.

Insured or self-funding: what you need before you book

The paperwork is different depending on how you are paying, and getting it right before the day saves a lot of back and forth.

If you have private medical insurance

Most insurers want a GP referral and a pre-authorisation number before the first appointment. Ring your insurer, tell them you have a breast symptom and that you would like to see me at The Holly, and they will give you the number. I am recognised by the major insurers, including Bupa, AXA Health, Aviva, Vitality and WPA.

If you are with Bupa, I am a Bupa Platinum consultant and fee-assured, so you should not face a shortfall on my fees for treatment your policy covers. I am also in their Open Referral network. If your GP gives you an open referral, which names the specialty rather than a surgeon, Bupa can still direct you to me. There is more on how that works in my post on Bupa Open Referral for breast surgery.

If you are paying for yourself

You do not need a GP referral. You can book directly, and the hospital confirms the consultation fee when you book. If scans or a biopsy are needed, those are quoted separately, and nothing beyond the consultation is done without your agreement. With your permission, I always write to your GP afterwards so your NHS record stays complete.

If you are not sure whether to go private at all

That is a fair question, and I would never want anyone to feel they have to pay to be seen. If your GP thinks your symptom could be cancer, the NHS will see you on an urgent suspected cancer pathway, and at Whipps Cross that means a one-stop clinic too. I have set out the honest differences in the two-week wait versus a private breast clinic.

How to book

There are a few routes, and patients choose whichever suits them.

  • My private secretary, who is based at The Holly. This is the best route if you have questions about insurance, want a particular day, or are not sure which clinic suits you.
  • Nuffield Health The Holly directly, either online or through the hospital's appointments line.
  • Doctify or Top Doctors, if you prefer to pick a slot online.
  • Bupa Finder, if you are a Bupa member.

Contact details and links for each are on my booking page. When you call, it helps to say that you have a new breast symptom, as new symptoms are given priority.

What to bring: your referral letter and pre-authorisation number if you are insured, any previous mammogram or breast ultrasound reports, and a list of your medicines. If you might need a mammogram, avoid deodorant or talcum powder on the day.

After the appointment

Scan findings are usually explained before you leave. Biopsy results normally take a few working days, and I go through them with you myself, either in clinic or by phone.

If you need treatment, you stay with me. The Holly has full operating theatres, so many operations, from removing a benign lump to breast cancer surgery, can be done at the same hospital, close to home. If your care is better suited to central London, I also see patients at Bupa's Wharfside Health Centre in Canary Wharf and operate at Nuffield Health St Bartholomew's, and I will talk that through with you.

If you have a new lump, a change in the skin, or a change in the nipple, please do not wait for a convenient appointment. See your GP or book promptly. Most breast symptoms turn out to be benign, but every one deserves a proper assessment.

Questions

Where is the nearest private breast clinic to Woodford Green?

Nuffield Health The Holly Hospital on the High Road in Buckhurst Hill, IG9 5HX. For most people in Woodford Green, Woodford Wells and South Woodford it is a short drive, with parking on site.

Do I need a GP referral to see a breast surgeon at The Holly?

Not if you are paying for yourself. Most insurers ask for a GP referral and a pre-authorisation number before the first appointment.

Which days does Mr Ullah see patients at The Holly?

Tuesday afternoons, when I run the Rapid Access Breast Clinic, and Thursday mornings.

Can I see Mr Ullah privately if he has treated me on the NHS at Whipps Cross?

Yes. Many of my private patients at The Holly first met me at Whipps Cross, and you can book in the usual way.
