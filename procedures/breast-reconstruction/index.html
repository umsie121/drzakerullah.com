Breast reconstruction

Reconstruction restores the shape of the breast after cancer surgery — at the same operation or years later. Mr Ullah offers the full range of implant-based and flap techniques, and is equally clear when no reconstruction is the right answer.

Immediate or delayed

Immediate reconstruction is performed during the same operation as the mastectomy, so the breast mound is restored before you wake. Delayed reconstruction can be done months or years later — there is no expiry date. The right choice depends on the cancer treatment planned (radiotherapy in particular), your health and your own preference. Some women choose no reconstruction at all; that is a valid choice, and Mr Ullah will say so.

Implant-based reconstruction

The most common technique in the UK uses a silicone implant, sometimes supported by a mesh or tissue matrix, to recreate the breast mound. It avoids surgery elsewhere on the body and has a shorter recovery, at the cost of a less natural feel and the likelihood of maintenance surgery over a lifetime.

Flap reconstruction

Reconstruction with your own tissue gives a warm, natural breast that ages with you. Mr Ullah performs latissimus dorsi (LD) flap reconstruction, which brings muscle and skin from the back around to the chest, often combined with an implant. For suitable central tumours he also uses the Grisotti flap, an elegant technique that reconstructs the nipple area using the breast's own tissue at the time of cancer surgery.

Finishing touches

Reconstruction is often completed in stages: fat grafting to refine contour, nipple reconstruction with a small local flap, and symmetrisation surgery to the other breast — a reduction, uplift or augmentation — so that the pair sit naturally together.

This page is general information, not medical advice. Whether a procedure is right for you can only be decided in consultation, after proper assessment.

FAQ

Common questions

Will a reconstructed breast look and feel natural?

Reconstruction restores shape and symmetry in clothing very reliably, and modern techniques can look natural out of clothing too. Sensation is usually reduced. Tissue-based reconstructions generally feel more natural than implants; Mr Ullah will show you representative outcomes for each option.

Is reconstruction safe if I need radiotherapy?

Radiotherapy increases complication rates for some reconstruction types, particularly implants. If radiotherapy is likely, that shapes the recommended technique or timing — one reason reconstruction is planned alongside cancer treatment rather than separately.

Can I have reconstruction years after my mastectomy?

Yes. Delayed reconstruction is routine and there is no time limit. Patients treated a decade or more ago still have options, subject to fitness and previous treatment.

Is breast reconstruction covered by insurance?

Reconstruction after breast cancer is normally covered as part of cancer treatment by UK private medical insurers, including symmetrising surgery to the other breast. Always confirm with your insurer in advance.
