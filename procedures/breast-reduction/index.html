Breast reduction & symmetrisation

Reduction mammoplasty reshapes heavy breasts to a lighter, higher, more comfortable form — for relief of symptoms, for proportion, or to balance the breasts after cancer surgery.

Book a consultation FAQs
Written and medically reviewed by Mr Md Zaker Ullah FRCS, Consultant Oncoplastic Breast Surgeon · Updated 3 November 2026

Who benefits

Heavy breasts are a physical burden: neck, shoulder and back pain, bra-strap grooving, skin irritation beneath the breast fold and real limits on exercise. A reduction removes excess tissue and skin, lifts the breast and repositions the nipple — exchanging weight and discomfort for scars that fade substantially over the first one to two years.

Symmetrisation after cancer surgery

As an oncoplastic surgeon, a large part of Mr Ullah's reduction practice is symmetrisation — reducing, lifting or reshaping the opposite breast so it matches a breast treated for cancer or reconstructed. This is a routine part of the reconstructive pathway and is usually covered by insurers as part of cancer treatment.

The operation and recovery

Breast reduction is performed under general anaesthetic and typically takes two to three hours, as a day case or with a single night in hospital. Most patients are back at desk work within one to two weeks and exercising fully at around six weeks. Mr Ullah discusses scar patterns, nipple sensation and the implications for breastfeeding candidly before any decision is made.

FAQ

Common questions

Will breast reduction affect nipple sensation or breastfeeding?

Both can be affected. Some change in nipple sensation is common early on and usually improves, though it can be permanent. Many women can still breastfeed after a reduction, but it cannot be guaranteed — if future breastfeeding matters to you, say so, as it influences technique and timing.

What scars does a breast reduction leave?

The usual pattern is a scar around the areola, a vertical line down to the breast fold, and often a line within the fold itself (the ‘anchor’ pattern). Scars are typically red and firm at first and fade substantially over 12–24 months.

Is breast reduction covered by insurance?

Cosmetic reduction generally is not, but symmetrisation after breast cancer surgery is usually treated as part of the reconstruction pathway. Your insurer confirms cover in advance either way.

How long is recovery after breast reduction?

Most women return to desk work within one to two weeks, drive at around two weeks once comfortable, and return to full exercise at about six weeks. A supportive bra is worn day and night for several weeks.
